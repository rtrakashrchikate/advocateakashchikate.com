Common Questions

Frequently Asked Legal Questions

Answers to the most common questions about criminal law, bail, divorce, and cybercrime in India.

Bail (Section 439 CrPC / BNSS) is applied for after arrest to secure release from custody. Anticipatory bail (Section 438 CrPC / 482 BNSS) is applied for before arrest when a person apprehends arrest. Anticipatory bail is pre-emptive protection — if granted, you cannot be arrested without prior notice to your lawyer. Regular bail is the post-arrest remedy to be released from jail pending trial.

Yes. The Bombay High Court has inherent powers under Section 482 CrPC (Section 528 BNSS) to quash an FIR. Common grounds include: the FIR is filed with malicious intent, the allegations are frivolous or do not constitute an offence, the dispute is civil in nature, or the parties have settled the matter. A qualified criminal lawyer can assess your case and file a writ petition at the Bombay High Court.

Mutual consent divorce (Section 13B HMA) typically takes 6 to 18 months, including the mandatory 6-month cooling-off period (which courts may waive). Contested divorce under Section 13 HMA can take 2–5 years or longer depending on the complexity of the case, number of hearings, and cooperation of both parties. An experienced family lawyer can significantly streamline the process.

Do not panic and do not ignore the notice. Immediately: (1) Do not delete any digital data, devices, or communications. (2) Do not share any passwords or login credentials with anyone. (3) Do not make any public statements online. (4) Contact a cyber law advocate immediately. Provide only legally required information and preserve all communication records. Early legal intervention can make a significant difference in the outcome.

Yes. The POSH Act 2013 applies to all employers with 10 or more employees — including private companies, startups, NGOs, and government organisations. You are legally required to: (1) Formulate an anti-sexual harassment policy, (2) Constitute an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC), (3) Conduct annual training and awareness programmes, and (4) Submit an annual compliance report. Failure can result in fines of ₹50,000 and cancellation of business licences.

Under Article 22 of the Constitution and Section 50 CrPC: (1) You have the right to be informed of the grounds of arrest. (2) You have the right to consult and be represented by a lawyer. (3) You have the right to be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours. (4) You must not sign any document or make any statement without your lawyer present. Call a criminal lawyer immediately — the first 24 hours are critical.

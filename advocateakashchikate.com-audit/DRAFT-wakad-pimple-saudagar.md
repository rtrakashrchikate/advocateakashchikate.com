### New section (place after "Local procedure")

> **Arrested or called by Wakad Police: the first 24 hours**
>
> 1. **Ask why.** The police must tell the arrested person the grounds of arrest (Section 47 BNSS) and inform a relative or friend (Section 48 BNSS). Ask for the FIR number and the sections applied.
> 2. **A notice is not an arrest.** A notice under Section 35 BNSS (old 41A CrPC) asks you to appear and cooperate. Read the date and the officer's name, keep a copy, and take advice before you reply or attend.
> 3. **Production before a Magistrate.** An arrested person must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours (Section 58 BNSS). That is the remand hearing, where the police may ask for custody and the defence can oppose it.
> 4. **Which court.** For Wakad Police Station cases, the remand court is named in the remand papers. Do not assume it is Pimpri or Shivajinagar; check the papers or ask the investigating officer.
> 5. **Bail.** In bailable offences, bail can be given at the police station. In non-bailable offences, the application goes to the Magistrate (Section 480 BNSS) or the Sessions Court (Section 483 BNSS). Where an arrest is only feared, anticipatory bail is sought under Section 482 BNSS.
> 6. **What to collect.** FIR copy or number, the arrest memo, any notice received, identity and address proof of the accused and of a surety, and a short note of dates.
>
> *This is general information, not advice on your matter.*

### Two questions to add to "Common Questions"

> **I received a call from Wakad Police asking me to come to the station. Do I have to go?**
> A phone call is not a formal notice. You can ask for a written notice under Section 35 BNSS stating the case and the date. If a written notice is served, it must be complied with, and failing to do so can lead to arrest. Take advice before attending.

> **Arrested or called by Sangvi Police: the first 24 hours**

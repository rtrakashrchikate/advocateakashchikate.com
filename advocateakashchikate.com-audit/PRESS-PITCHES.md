## Pitch 2: Proclamation against an "absconding" MCOCA accused stayed because an anticipatory bail application was already pending

**Subject:** Filing anticipatory bail shows recourse to law, not absconding: Bombay High Court stays MCOCA proclamation, [case no.], [date] July 2025

> Dear Editor,
>
> Attached is the order of the Bombay High Court (Justice S.M. Modak) dated [date] July 2025 in [case title and number].
>
> The petitioner was shown as an absconding accused in an MCOCA chargesheet registered at Kothrud Police Station, Pune, and the Special Judge issued a proclamation. In a writ petition, the High Court observed that the petitioner had already filed an anticipatory bail application, of which the police were aware through their own report. That showed he had taken recourse to law, making the "absconding" label untenable. The Court kept the proclamation in abeyance and directed the Special Judge to hear the anticipatory bail application on priority.
>
> The point, that a pending anticipatory bail application undercuts a proclamation under Section 84 BNSS (old Section 82 CrPC), is likely to be useful to practitioners.
>
> I appeared for the petitioner. The order is attached.

## Pitch 3: Murder conviction altered to Section 304 Part I after 11 years in custody

**Subject:** Bombay High Court alters murder conviction to culpable homicide (sudden fight) and releases appellant after 11 years: [case no.], [date] August 2025

> Dear Editor,
>
> Attached is the judgment of a Division Bench of the Bombay High Court (Sarang V. Kotwal and Advait M. Sethna, JJ.) dated [date] August 2025 in [case title and number].
>
> The appellant had been convicted under Section 302 IPC and sentenced to life imprisonment for pushing a person from a railway skywalk at Kandivali. He had been in custody since June 2014. The Bench found that the prosecution had not explained significant injuries on the appellant, had established no motive or prior enmity, and that the evidence pointed to a sudden fight. Holding that the case fell within Exception 4 to Section 300 IPC (now Exception 4 to Section 101 BNS), the Court altered the conviction to Section 304 Part I IPC (now Section 105 BNS), reduced the sentence to the period already undergone and directed his release.

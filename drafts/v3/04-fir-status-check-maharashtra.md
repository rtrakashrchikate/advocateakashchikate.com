<h2>Step 1: Check the arrest and FIR records online</h2>
<p>The Maharashtra Police citizen portal (citizen.mahapolice.gov.in) has an Arrested Person Search. You enter a date range, and can add name, age range, district, police station, gender and nationality. The page says arrested and surrendered person data is available from 26 June 2015. The Mumbai Police and Pimpri-Chinchwad Police websites link to it.</p>
<p>The published-FIR search needs a date range of under 90 days, and sensitive FIRs are not published. It shows the FIR as registered. It does not show what happened later. Details are in our <a href="/how-to-check-fir-registered-against-you-maharashtra/">FIR search guide</a>.</p>
<h2>Step 2: Ask about the investigation</h2>
<p>The Maharashtra Police websites list no public tracker for investigation progress. The practical route is the police station that registered the FIR, and its investigating officer (IO).</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Write, do not only phone.</strong> Give the FIR number, year, police station and date. Ask for the stage of the case. Keep the stamped acknowledgement.</li>
<li><strong>Informants and victims have a statutory right.</strong> Section 193(3)(ii) BNSS says the police officer shall, within ninety days, inform the informant or the victim of the progress of the investigation. This can be by any means, including electronic communication.</li>
<li><strong>Complainants who used the portal.</strong> If you filed through the citizen portal, check your login for updates. The public pages do not describe what the login shows.</li>
<li><strong>Accused persons.</strong> The 90-day provision names the informant or victim, not the accused. An accused usually learns the stage from a notice, an arrest, a chargesheet or a court summons.</li>
</ul>
<p>A request under the Right to Information Act is another route. The Maharashtra Police website has an RTI page. Section 8(1)(h) of that Act exempts information that would impede the process of investigation, so details may be refused while an investigation is pending.</p>
<h2>Step 3: Search eCourts after the chargesheet</h2>
<p>The investigation ends with a police report, known as the chargesheet. Section 193 BNSS (old Section 173 CrPC) requires it to be sent to a Magistrate. Once the court registers a case, you can search the eCourts Services website (services.ecourts.gov.in) or the eCourts Services app. Select Maharashtra, the district and the court complex. Then use one of these.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>CNR number.</strong> Enter the 16-character CNR number without hyphens or spaces, and complete the captcha. It shows the current status and history of the case. If you do not have the number, ask your advocate or the court office.</li>
<li><strong>FIR number.</strong> Under Case Status, the FIR Number option asks for the police station. The FIR number and year are optional. You can filter by pending, disposed or both.</li>
<li><strong>Case number, party name, advocate name, filing number, act type or case type.</strong> The Case Status page offers these as separate searches.</li>
</ol>
<p>A case appears only after the court registers it, so a search made soon after a chargesheet may show nothing. Name searches can return many results, so match the parties, case number and court. If the case was filed in Pune or Mumbai, try the courts of that district.</p>
<h2>Step 4: Bombay High Court case status</h2>
<p>The eCourts High Court Services website (hcservices.ecourts.gov.in) lists the Bombay High Court. It tells you to first select the High Court and the Bench to which the case belongs. Cases can be searched by party name, case number, filing number, advocate name, FIR number, act or case type, and by CNR number. Criminal matters from Pune are heard in Mumbai. There is no Pune bench.</p>

<h2>What each stage means</h2>
<p>Section numbers below are BNSS, with the old CrPC number in brackets. A case based on an offence before 1 July 2024 follows the old CrPC. Our <a href="/crpc-to-bnss-ipc-to-bns-section-table/">section table</a> lists both.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>FIR.</strong> The police record information of a cognizable offence under Section 173 (old 154). For some offences carrying three to under seven years, a preliminary enquiry of up to 14 days may come first under Section 173(3).</li>
<li><strong>Investigation.</strong> The police collect evidence and record statements. Under Section 35 (old 41 and 41A) they may arrest, or issue a notice to attend. See <a href="/practice-areas/police-notice-section-35-bnss/">police notice under Section 35 BNSS</a> and <a href="/rights-when-arrested-india/">rights when arrested</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Arrest and remand.</strong> A Magistrate can authorise custody under Section 187 (old 167). The total detention period is limited to 90 days for offences punishable with death, life or ten years or more, and 60 days for others. After that, the accused is entitled to be released on bail if prepared to furnish it. See <a href="/practice-areas/default-bail-undertrial-release/">default bail</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Chargesheet.</strong> The police report under Section 193 (old 173) states, among other things, whether an offence appears to have been committed and by whom. Further investigation can continue after it, under Section 193(9). What follows is explained in <a href="/chargesheet-filed-what-next/">chargesheet filed: what next</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Cognizance.</strong> A Magistrate may take cognizance upon a police report under Section 210(1)(b) (old 190).</li>
<li><strong>Documents to the accused.</strong> Section 230 (old 207) requires the Magistrate to give the accused a copy of the police report, the FIR and the listed statements. This must be without delay, and in no case beyond fourteen days from production or appearance.</li>
<li><strong>Committal, discharge and charge.</strong> If the offence is triable only by the Court of Session, the Magistrate commits the case under Section 232 (old 209). The accused may apply for discharge within sixty days of commitment under Section 250 (old 227). See <a href="/practice-areas/discharge-application/">discharge application</a> and <a href="/practice-areas/sessions-trial-defence/">Sessions Court trial</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Trial.</strong> Section 346 (old 309) says proceedings continue day to day until the witnesses in attendance are examined, unless the court records reasons to adjourn.</li>
</ol>
<p>A chargesheet is not a finding of guilt. It is the police version, tested in court.</p>
<h2>If there is no progress</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Put it in writing.</strong> Write to the IO and then the Senior Police Inspector, with your FIR details and earlier requests.</li>
<li><strong>Use the grievance channels.</strong> The Pimpri-Chinchwad Police website lists a Complaint Resolution Day at police stations on Saturdays, 10:30 AM to 2:00 PM, with the DCP, ACP and station officers attending. It also links to the Aaple Sarkar and PG Portal grievance sites. The Mumbai Police website has a page on Deputy Commissioner of Police visits. Check each site for current timings.</li>
<li><strong>Remember the statutory duties.</strong> Section 193(1) says every investigation shall be completed without unnecessary delay, and the 90-day progress duty applies to the informant or victim.</li>
<li><strong>For a refusal to register.</strong> This is a different problem. See our guide on <a href="/file-fir-online-maharashtra-pune-mumbai/">how to file an FIR online in Maharashtra</a>.</li>
<li><strong>If you are the accused and in custody.</strong> Check the 60 or 90 day period and any default bail right with an advocate.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>How do I check FIR status online in Maharashtra?</h3>
<p>You can search the published FIR and the arrested-person record on the Maharashtra Police citizen portal. Investigation progress must be asked from the police station. After a chargesheet, search eCourts.</p>
<h3>Is there a Maharashtra FIR status tracker by FIR number?</h3>
<p>The police websites checked list no public tracker for investigation status. On eCourts you can search by FIR number, police station and year, but only for cases that a court has registered.</p>
<h3>How do I check Mumbai FIR status?</h3>
<p>The Mumbai Police website links to the published-FIR and arrested-person searches on the state portal. For progress, contact the police station. For a court case, search eCourts with the FIR number, police station and year, or the CNR number.</p>
<h3>What is a CNR number, and how do I use it?</h3>
<p>It is a 16-character code that identifies a case on eCourts. Enter it without hyphens or spaces on the eCourts Services page, complete the captcha, and the page shows the current status and history.</p>
<h3>Can I check FIR status in Pune without going to the police station?</h3>
<p>You can check the published FIR and arrest records online. Progress details usually need a request to the police station. Informants and victims can expect a progress update within ninety days under Section 193(3)(ii) BNSS.</p>
<h3>How do I check my case in the Bombay High Court?</h3>
<p>Use eCourts High Court Services. Select the High Court and Bench, then search by case number, party name, FIR number or CNR. The High Court sits in Mumbai for Pune matters.</p>
<h3>Does a chargesheet mean the case is over?</h3>
<p>No. It starts the court stage. The Magistrate may take cognizance, the accused receives the documents, and the case goes to trial or, in Sessions cases, to committal, discharge and charge.</p>

<p><strong>In short:</strong> In Maharashtra you can start a police complaint online through the Maharashtra Police citizen portal, which the Mumbai Police and Pimpri-Chinchwad Police websites link to. Under Section 173(1) BNSS, information sent by electronic communication is taken on record only after you sign it within three days. For serious offences, plan to go to a police station. If the police refuse to record your information, the law gives you two further steps.</p>
<h2>Online complaint, e-FIR and FIR: what is the difference</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>FIR (First Information Report):</strong> The written record of information about a cognizable offence. A cognizable offence is one where police can usually arrest without a warrant, such as theft or assault. It is recorded under Section 173 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), which replaced Section 154 CrPC on 1 July 2024.</li>
<li><strong>e-FIR:</strong> Information about a cognizable offence given by electronic communication. Section 173(1)(ii) BNSS allows this, with a signature condition explained below.</li>
<li><strong>Online complaint (e-complaint):</strong> A message to the police through a website form. It may lead to an FIR, a non-cognizable (NC) entry, or no action.</li>
</ul>
<p>An offence committed before 1 July 2024 is still dealt with under the old CrPC and the Indian Penal Code. Our <a href="/crpc-to-bnss-ipc-to-bns-section-table/">section table</a> lists the old and new numbers together.</p>

<h3>The Mumbai Police Online Complaint form</h3>
<p>The Mumbai Police form states that it accepts only complaints about minor crimes. For major crimes it says an FIR can be registered only at a police station. Its list includes theft, burglary, assault, rape, murder, robbery and extortion.</p>
<p>The form asks for your name, contact number, email, the police station and the complaint text. You can attach files of up to 3 MB each and must certify that the information is correct. The complaint goes to the station you select, which may contact you or record your statement.</p>
<h3>The Maharashtra Police citizen portal</h3>
<p>The portal sits on the national CCTNS system. Its login page uses a username, password and captcha, and a mobile OTP step. The public pages do not say which offences the online option accepts. If you are unsure, go to the station.</p>
<p>A November 2025 news report on a government review meeting gives 958 e-FIRs and 12,398 Zero FIRs for Maharashtra between July 2024 and September 2025. It does not say which offences the e-FIRs covered.</p>
<p>In an emergency, call 112, which the Maharashtra Police site lists as the police hotline. The national cyber-crime portal is a separate system and is not covered here.</p>
<h2>Steps to file a complaint online</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Collect the facts first.</strong> Write down date, time, place, names, phone numbers and what happened, in order. Keep documents and screenshots ready.</li>
<li><strong>Choose the channel.</strong> Use the Mumbai Police form for a minor matter in Mumbai. Use the citizen portal login (create a login with your mobile number) for other matters.</li>
<li><strong>Select the right police station.</strong> Pick the station for the area where it happened. If you are not sure, see Zero FIR below.</li>
<li><strong>Describe the incident and attach documents.</strong> Stick to facts. Do not add names you cannot support.</li>
<li><strong>Save the acknowledgement or reference number.</strong> Take a screenshot.</li>
<li><strong>Sign and follow up.</strong> For information about a cognizable offence, plan to visit the station and sign within three days.</li>
</ol>
<h2>The three-day signing rule</h2>
<p>Section 173(1)(ii) BNSS says information given by electronic communication "shall be taken on record by him on being signed within three days by the person giving it." Sources differ on what happens if you miss the three days, and the text does not say. Visit the station within three days and ask for the signed copy.</p>
<p>Under Section 173(2), a copy of the recorded information must be given forthwith, free of cost, to the informant or the victim. Section 173(1) also covers a woman reporting certain offences against her, such as sexual offences under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS). A woman police officer or woman officer must record that information.</p>
<h2>Zero FIR: if the offence happened in another area</h2>
<p>Section 173(1) BNSS covers information about a cognizable offence "irrespective of the area where the offence is committed." This is commonly called a Zero FIR. A station in Pune can record it even if the offence happened elsewhere. Police then pass the case to the station with jurisdiction.</p>
<h2>What happens after you file</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>FIR number and copy.</strong> Note the FIR number, year and police station. You can also look it up using our guide on <a href="/how-to-check-fir-registered-against-you-maharashtra/">how to check an FIR in Maharashtra</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Preliminary enquiry.</strong> Where the offence is punishable with three years or more but less than seven, Section 173(3) BNSS lets the station officer hold a preliminary enquiry of up to fourteen days. This needs prior permission from an officer not below Deputy Superintendent of Police. In <em>Imran Pratapgadhi v. State of Gujarat</em> (Supreme Court, 28 March 2025, Justices Abhay S. Oka and Ujjal Bhuyan), the Court held that Section 173(3) is an exception to Section 173(1). If a prima facie case exists, the officer should register the FIR immediately. If not, he should immediately inform the informant, who can then use Section 173(4).</li>
<li><strong>Progress reports.</strong> Section 193(3)(ii) BNSS requires the police to inform the informant or the victim of the progress of the investigation within ninety days. See our guide on <a href="/fir-status-check-maharashtra-pune-mumbai/">FIR status check in Maharashtra</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>Earlier, in <em>Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh</em> (Constitution Bench, 12 November 2013), the Supreme Court held that the police must register an FIR when the information discloses a cognizable offence. It allowed a limited preliminary inquiry only in certain categories, such as family disputes and commercial offences.</p>
<h2>If the police refuse to record your information</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Section 173(4) BNSS.</strong> Send the substance of the information in writing, by post, to the Superintendent of Police concerned. If satisfied that it discloses a cognizable offence, the Superintendent must investigate or direct an investigation. In Pune and Mumbai, ask the station which senior officer receives such a letter.</li>
<li><strong>Section 175(3) BNSS.</strong> If that fails, you may apply to the Magistrate. The application must be supported by an affidavit. The Magistrate makes the inquiry he thinks necessary, considers the police officer's submission, and may then order an investigation.</li>
</ol>
<p>The old CrPC equivalents were Sections 154(3) and 156(3). For a dispute that is really private, such as a cheque or a civil claim, a complaint to the Magistrate may fit better. See <a href="/private-complaint-section-200-crpc-section-223-bnss-pune/">private complaint under Section 223 BNSS</a>. A <a href="/practice-areas/criminal-defence/">criminal lawyer in Pune</a> can explain which route fits.</p>
<h2>Mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Filing an online complaint and assuming an FIR now exists. Check for an FIR number.</li>
<li>Missing the three-day signing window for an e-FIR.</li>
<li>Exaggerating or adding names. A false complaint can lead to action against the complainant.</li>
<li>Giving up after the first refusal. Use Zero FIR, then Sections 173(4) and 175(3).</li>
</ul>
<h2>If the complaint is false, or you are the person accused</h2>
<p>An FIR is only the complainant's version. It is not a finding. If you learn that a complaint names you, do not ignore a police call or notice. For offences where arrest is not required, the police must issue a notice under Section 35(3) BNSS (old Section 41A CrPC). See <a href="/practice-areas/police-notice-section-35-bnss/">police notice under Section 35 BNSS</a>.</p>
<p>The options include <a href="/practice-areas/anticipatory-bail/">anticipatory bail</a> under Section 482 BNSS (old Section 438 CrPC). Another is <a href="/practice-areas/fir-quashing/">quashing of the FIR</a> before the Bombay High Court under Section 528 BNSS (old Section 482 CrPC). The BNS also makes two false-complaint acts offences: false information to a public servant (Section 217, old IPC 182) and a false charge made with intent to injure (Section 248, old IPC 211). Our page on <a href="/false-fir-pune-legal-remedies/">remedies against a false FIR in Pune</a> explains these steps.</p>
<p>Matters before the Bombay High Court and Mumbai courts are handled, with consultation by phone or video. The office is in Shivajinagar, Pune. If you need advice on your situation, you can contact the office.</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Can I file an FIR online in Maharashtra?</h3>
<p>You can start a complaint online through the Maharashtra Police citizen portal or, in Mumbai, the Mumbai Police Online Complaint form. For information about a cognizable offence, Section 173(1)(ii) BNSS requires your signature within three days before it is taken on record. For serious offences, go to the station.</p>
<h3>Which offences can be reported through the Mumbai Police online form?</h3>
<p>The form says it accepts only complaints about minor crimes. It lists theft, burglary, assault, rape, murder, robbery and extortion among offences for which an FIR can be registered only at a police station.</p>
<h3>Is an online complaint the same as an FIR?</h3>
<p>No. It becomes an FIR when the police record the information under Section 173 BNSS and give it an FIR number. Ask for that number.</p>
<h3>What is a Zero FIR, and can I file one in Pune?</h3>
<p>It is an FIR recorded at any police station, whatever the area of the offence, under Section 173(1) BNSS. The case is then passed on to the station with jurisdiction.</p>
<h3>What if the police refuse to register my FIR?</h3>
<p>Send the substance of your information in writing, by post, to the Superintendent of Police under Section 173(4) BNSS. If that does not work, apply to the Magistrate under Section 175(3) with an affidavit. The old CrPC equivalents were Sections 154(3) and 156(3).</p>
<h3>Will the police always register an FIR immediately?</h3>
<p>Not always. For offences punishable with three years or more but less than seven, Section 173(3) BNSS allows a preliminary enquiry of up to fourteen days. It needs prior permission from a Deputy Superintendent of Police or above. Otherwise, the Supreme Court held in <em>Lalita Kumari</em> that registration is mandatory when the information discloses a cognizable offence, with limited exceptions.</p>
<h3>I got a notice after someone filed an online FIR against me. What now?</h3>
<p>Do not ignore it. Read the sections cited, note the date, time and place to appear, and take legal advice before you reply. Depending on the facts, options include anticipatory bail or quashing.</p>

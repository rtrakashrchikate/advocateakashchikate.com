<p><strong>In short:</strong> A bail application is a written petition asking a court to release a person. Regular bail (after arrest) is sought under Section 480 BNSS before a Magistrate, or Section 483 BNSS before the Sessions Court or High Court. Anticipatory bail (before arrest) is sought under Section 482 BNSS. Both follow the same basic parts, explained below with a sample structure.</p>

<p>The old numbers are Sections 437, 439 and 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), so many people search for a "bail application under CrPC". This page is not a ready-to-file draft. A real application must fit the facts of the case and be signed by an advocate.</p>

<h2>Bail and anticipatory bail: which application, which court</h2>
<table>
<tr><th>Application</th><th>When</th><th>Section (BNSS / old CrPC)</th><th>Court</th></tr>
<tr><td>Regular bail</td><td>After arrest</td><td>480 / 437</td><td>Magistrate court, Pune or Pimpri-Chinchwad</td></tr>
<tr><td>Regular bail</td><td>After arrest</td><td>483 / 439</td><td>Pune District and Sessions Court (Shivajinagar), or Bombay High Court, Mumbai</td></tr>
<tr><td>Anticipatory bail</td><td>Before arrest, for a non-bailable offence</td><td>482 / 438</td><td>Sessions Court or Bombay High Court</td></tr>
</table>
<p>Section 480 applies to courts other than the High Court and the Court of Session, so it covers Magistrates. The Sessions Court and High Court use Section 483. There is no Pune bench of the Bombay High Court, so High Court applications are filed in Mumbai.</p>
<p>For the law on each, see <a href="/practice-areas/bail-application/">bail application in Pune</a> and <a href="/practice-areas/anticipatory-bail/">anticipatory bail in Pune</a>.</p>

<h2>The parts of a bail application</h2>
<p>Each court has its own style. Most applications for bail contain these parts, in this order.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Court and cause title.</strong> The name of the court, the type and number of the application, and the parties. The accused is the applicant. The State of Maharashtra, through the police station, is the respondent.</li>
<li><strong>Applicant and FIR details.</strong> Name, age, address and occupation of the applicant. The FIR number and date, the police station, the sections applied, and the date of arrest if the person is in custody.</li>
<li><strong>Facts.</strong> A short, numbered account of the allegation and the applicant's own version. Only facts. No arguments here.</li>
<li><strong>Grounds.</strong> The reasons the court should grant bail, one numbered ground at a time. Typical grounds are the applicant's role, the stage of investigation, time in custody, fixed residence, health and no history of absconding.</li>
<li><strong>Earlier applications.</strong> Whether any earlier bail application was filed in this case, in which court, and what happened. This part is compulsory in practice, as explained below.</li>
<li><strong>Conditions the applicant will accept.</strong> For example, attending the police station or court when required, not contacting witnesses, and surrendering the passport if asked.</li>
<li><strong>Prayer.</strong> The exact order sought, such as release on bail on a stated type of bond, with an alternative prayer for any other suitable order.</li>
<li><strong>Verification or affidavit.</strong> A statement by the applicant, or a family member where the applicant is in custody, that the facts are true. The form varies by court.</li>
<li><strong>Annexures, list of documents and vakalatnama.</strong> Copies of the papers relied on, indexed and numbered. The vakalatnama is the document by which the applicant authorises the advocate to appear.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Disclose earlier bail applications: what the Supreme Court said</h2>
<p>In <em>Kusha Duruka v. State of Odisha</em> (19 January 2024, Justices Vikram Nath and Rajesh Bindal), the Supreme Court directed that bail applications must mandatorily state two things:</p>
<ul>
<li>details and copies of orders passed in the applicant's earlier bail applications that are already decided; and</li>
<li>details of any bail application of the applicant that is pending in a lower or higher court, or a clear statement that none is pending.</li>
</ul>
<p>The Court also said the top of the application should show whether it is the first, second or third. The case arose because an earlier rejection and a pending appeal were not mentioned. The Bombay High Court Registry reportedly issued a circular in February 2024 in line with this order (see Sources). Check the current circular before filing.</p>

<h2>Sample structure with blanks</h2>
<p>This is a skeleton for understanding, not a draft to copy. Square brackets show what must be filled from the case papers.</p>
<table>
<tr><th>Part</th><th>Skeleton (regular bail, Sessions Court)</th></tr>
<tr><td>Heading</td><td>IN THE COURT OF THE SESSIONS JUDGE, PUNE. Criminal Bail Application No. [ ] of [year]. This is the [first / second] bail application.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Parties</td><td>[Name], aged [ ], residing at [address]: Applicant (accused). Versus The State of Maharashtra, through [Police Station]: Respondent.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Subject</td><td>Application for regular bail under Section 483 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 in Crime No. [ ] of [year], registered at [Police Station], under Sections [ ] of [BNS / IPC].</td></tr>
<tr><td>Facts</td><td>1. FIR registered on [date]. 2. Applicant arrested on [date], in [police / judicial] custody. 3. Applicant's version: [ ].</td></tr>
<tr><td>Grounds</td><td>1. [Role alleged.] 2. [Stage of investigation or chargesheet.] 3. [Time in custody.] 4. [Residence, family, work.]</td></tr>
<tr><td>Earlier applications</td><td>[No earlier bail application was filed.] OR [Filed in [court] on [date], decided on [date]; order at Annexure [ ].] Pending in any court: [none / details].</td></tr>
<tr><td>Conditions and prayer</td><td>Applicant will follow conditions including [ ]. Prayer: release on bail in Crime No. [ ] on terms the Court thinks fit.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Signature and verification</td><td>[Advocate for the applicant]. [Place, date]. [Applicant's statement or affidavit, as the court requires.]</td></tr>
<tr><td>Annexures</td><td>A: FIR. B: Arrest memo. C: Remand order. D: Earlier bail order. E: ID and address proof.</td></tr>
</table>

<h2>What changes in an anticipatory bail application</h2>
<p>In <em>Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi)</em> (29 January 2020), a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court said the application should rest on concrete facts, not vague or general allegations. It should give the bare essential facts of the offence, why the applicant reasonably fears arrest, and the applicant's side of the story. An FIR need not exist if the facts are clear.</p>
<p>So the anticipatory bail skeleton differs from the one above in these places:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Subject:</strong> "Application under Section 482 BNSS (old Section 438 CrPC) for pre-arrest bail in Crime No. [ ] / in apprehension of arrest in [complaint or notice details]."</li>
<li><strong>Facts:</strong> add the date of the FIR or complaint, any police notice (see <a href="/practice-areas/police-notice-section-35-bnss/">Section 35 BNSS notice</a>), and why arrest is feared.</li>
<li><strong>Grounds:</strong> add cooperation with the police and ties to Pune.</li>
<li><strong>Prayer:</strong> release on bail if arrested in the named case, and short-term (interim) protection until the hearing. The Supreme Court said a court may give limited interim protection while issuing notice to the Public Prosecutor, so this prayer is common but not automatic. See <a href="/interim-bail-meaning-pune/">interim bail meaning</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Conditions:</strong> Section 482(2) lets the court direct that the applicant be available for questioning, not threaten or induce witnesses, and not leave India without the court's permission. It also allows the conditions in Section 480(3).</li>
</ul>
<p>More on this in <a href="/section-438-crpc-section-482-bnss-anticipatory-bail-explained/">Section 438 CrPC (Section 482 BNSS) explained</a> and <a href="/how-to-get-anticipatory-bail-pune/">how to get anticipatory bail in Pune</a>.</p>

<h2>Filing: Magistrate, Sessions Court, Bombay High Court</h2>
<p>The Magistrate or Sessions Court hears the application where the case is registered. The High Court application is filed in Mumbai. See <a href="/bombay-high-court-lawyer-pune/">Bombay High Court criminal matters</a>.</p>
<p>The e-Filing Rules of the High Court of Bombay, 2022 apply to the High Court and the district courts under it. Filing is online through the court portal, with digital or Aadhaar-based e-signatures, and signed vakalatnamas and affidavits must be kept for production later (Rules 3, 7 and 10.3). If the portal is down, Rule 14.3 allows designated counters or physical filing. The rules cover the case categories the High Court notifies, so ask the registry or your advocate whether a bail application must be e-filed.</p>
<p>Court staff may raise "office objections", such as missing annexures. The Pune District Court website lists an office objection list for criminal bail applications. Fixing these early avoids delay.</p>

<h2>Cases before 1 July 2024</h2>
<p>The BNSS replaced the CrPC on 1 July 2024. For an offence registered earlier, the case is still tried under the old laws, and the FIR will quote IPC sections. How a court numbers or cites a bail application for such a case can vary. The tests for bail stayed the same. See the <a href="/crpc-to-bnss-ipc-to-bns-section-table/">CrPC to BNSS section table</a>.</p>

<h2>Mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Copying a downloaded format without changing the facts, sections and dates.</li>
<li>Leaving out an earlier rejected application, or not stating that none is pending.</li>
<li>Filing in the wrong court, or seeking anticipatory bail after arrest.</li>
<li>General grounds with no dates, documents or address proof.</li>
<li>Offering conditions the applicant cannot follow.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Is there a bail application format or PDF I can download and file myself?</h3>
<p>Formats are easy to find, but a court looks at the facts, sections and grounds of your case. A copied draft often misses these. The office does not provide a fill-in form.</p>
<h3>What is the difference between a bail application and an anticipatory bail application?</h3>
<p>A bail application is filed after arrest. An anticipatory bail application is filed before arrest, in a non-bailable case. The first is under Section 480 or 483 BNSS, the second under Section 482 BNSS.</p>
<h3>Which Section is a bail application under now: 437, 438 or 439 CrPC?</h3>
<p>Section 437 CrPC is now Section 480 BNSS (Magistrate). Section 438 CrPC is now Section 482 BNSS (anticipatory bail). Section 439 CrPC is now Section 483 BNSS (Sessions Court and High Court).</p>
<h3>What is "court bail paper" or "court bail form"?</h3>
<p>People use these terms for the bail application, and sometimes for the bail bond signed after bail is granted. The application asks for release. The bond and surety papers come after the court grants it.</p>
<h3>Do I have to mention my earlier bail applications?</h3>
<p>Yes. The Supreme Court directed in Kusha Duruka v. State of Odisha that details and orders of earlier applications, and any pending application, must be stated. Not doing so can harm the application.</p>
<h3>Can the application be e-filed in Maharashtra?</h3>
<p>The Bombay High Court has e-Filing Rules (2022) covering the High Court and district courts. Whether a bail application must be e-filed depends on the court's notified categories. Ask the registry or your advocate.</p>

<p><strong>In short:</strong> A Look Out Circular (LOC) is an alert sent to immigration counters that can stop a person from leaving India. It is opened on the request of a listed government agency or on the direction of a criminal court. An FIR alone does not automatically create an LOC. If you are stopped at the airport, you can ask the agency that opened it to withdraw it, apply to the criminal court, or file a writ petition in the Bombay High Court.</p>

<h2>What is a Look Out Circular?</h2>
<p>An LOC is not a law passed by Parliament. It works through Office Memorandums (OMs) issued by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA). The Bureau of Immigration (BoI) enforces it at airports, seaports and land checkposts.</p>
<p>The key documents are:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>OM dated 27 October 2010:</strong> the first detailed guidelines. It followed Delhi High Court orders in 2010, including the judgment in <em>Sumer Singh Salkan v. Asstt. Director</em>.</li>
<li><strong>OM dated 22 February 2021:</strong> the current "consolidated guidelines". It replaced all the earlier letters and OMs.</li>
</ul>
<p>Under the 2021 guidelines, a person can be detained or stopped only in a cognizable offence (earlier under the IPC, now the BNS or other penal laws). Otherwise, the agency can only ask to be told when the person travels.</p>
<p>The 2010 OM said an LOC lapsed after one year unless renewed. The 2021 OM says it stays in force until the agency that opened it asks for deletion.</p>

<h2>Who can ask for an LOC?</h2>
<p>Under clause 6(B) of the 2021 OM, the request must be approved by an officer of a listed rank. The list includes a district Superintendent of Police, a District Magistrate, and officers of the CBI, ED, NIA, NCB, income tax, customs and others. A criminal court can also direct that an LOC be opened.</p>
<p>Statutory bodies such as the National Commission for Women cannot ask for an LOC directly. They must pass the facts to the police, who decide.</p>
<p><strong>Bank LOCs:</strong> The 2021 list also included the heads of public sector banks. On 23 April 2024, the Bombay High Court in <em>Viraj Chetan Shah v. Union of India</em> struck down that part and quashed the bank-requested LOCs. It said restraint orders from a court, tribunal (such as the DRT) or investigating agency are not affected. A Bombay High Court order of November 2025 records that the issue is pending before the Supreme Court.</p>
<p>News reports from March 2026 say the MHA has revised parts of the guidelines again. Check the latest official text before relying on any single clause.</p>

<h2>Does an FIR mean there is automatically an LOC?</h2>
<p>No. An LOC must be specifically requested and approved. In <em>Sumer Singh Salkan</em>, the Delhi High Court said an LOC is meant for cognizable offences where the accused is deliberately evading arrest or not appearing in court despite non-bailable warrants, and is likely to leave the country.</p>
<p>In practice, people who answer police notices and attend court are in a stronger position. If you are unsure whether there is a case at all, see <a href="/how-to-check-fir-registered-against-you-maharashtra/">how to check if an FIR is registered against you in Maharashtra</a>.</p>

<h2>How people find out: usually at the airport</h2>
<p>There is no official public website to search for an LOC. The guidelines do not require the agency to tell you in advance. Most people, including NRIs and IT professionals flying back to work, learn about it at the immigration counter.</p>
<p>If you are stopped, stay calm. Immigration staff only act on the LOC. Ask politely which agency opened it, note any reference given, and contact a lawyer and your family. If the LOC says "detain", the agency may take custody.</p>

<h2>How to challenge an LOC: your options</h2>
<p>The Delhi High Court in <em>Sumer Singh Salkan</em> set out the basic remedies. They are still followed:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Join the investigation or appear in court.</strong> Appearing before the investigating officer, or before the court, often removes the reason for the LOC.</li>
<li><strong>Representation to the originating agency.</strong> You can write to the officer who ordered the LOC and explain why it was wrongly issued. The agency can withdraw it.</li>
<li><strong>Application to the trial court (Magistrate or Sessions Court).</strong> The court where the case is pending, or which has jurisdiction over the police station, can cancel ("rescind") the LOC, much as it can cancel a non-bailable warrant. In Pune this is the Magistrate court or the Sessions Court at Shivajinagar. See also <a href="/non-bailable-warrant-cancellation-pune/">cancelling a non-bailable warrant in Pune</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Writ petition in the Bombay High Court under Article 226.</strong> This is used where the LOC has no legal basis, was issued by an authority with no power, or is unreasonable. The right to travel abroad is part of personal liberty under Article 21, as the Supreme Court held in <em>Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India</em>. Petitions are heard at the High Court in Mumbai; there is no Pune bench.</li>
</ol>
<p>If the FIR itself is false or abusive, quashing it removes the base of the LOC. Read about <a href="/fir-quashing-bombay-high-court-pune/">FIR quashing in the Bombay High Court</a> under Section 528 BNSS (old Section 482 CrPC).</p>
<p><strong>A practical point:</strong> clause 6(K) of the 2021 OM says immigration cannot verify court orders on its own. Orders deleting or suspending an LOC must reach the BoI through the same agency that opened it. Carry a certified copy, but also make sure the police or agency has actually informed the BoI before you book a flight.</p>

<h2>Travelling abroad while on bail</h2>
<p>This is a separate issue from an LOC. Many bail orders say you cannot leave India without the court's permission. For anticipatory bail, Section 482(2)(iii) BNSS (old Section 438(2)(iii) CrPC) allows exactly this condition. Regular bail orders under Section 480 BNSS (old Section 437 CrPC) or Section 483 BNSS (old Section 439 CrPC) often contain it too.</p>
<p>If your order has this condition, you must apply to the court before travelling. Usually this is the court that granted bail or the court where the trial is pending. Courts may attach conditions, such as a fixed return date or a deposit. Read more on <a href="/anticipatory-bail-conditions-pune/">anticipatory bail conditions in Pune</a>.</p>
<p>Travelling without permission can lead to cancellation of bail. It can also lead the court or police to seek an LOC.</p>

<h2>Passport with a pending criminal case</h2>
<p>Section 6(2)(f) of the Passports Act, 1967 lets the passport authority refuse a passport if criminal proceedings against the applicant are pending before a criminal court in India. But this is not the end of the matter:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>FIR stage:</strong> An MEA Office Memorandum dated 10 October 2019 says mere filing of an FIR, or a case under investigation, does not come under Section 6(2)(f). A case is "pending" once a court has taken cognizance. High Courts have applied this.</li>
<li><strong>With court permission:</strong> MEA notification GSR 570(E) dated 25 August 1993 allows a passport if the criminal court permits it and you give an undertaking to appear. If the court does not state a period, the passport is usually issued for one year.</li>
<li><strong>Supreme Court, December 2025:</strong> In <em>Mahesh Kumar Agarwal v. Union of India</em>, the Supreme Court said Section 6(2)(f) is not an absolute bar. Where the criminal courts permit renewal, the passport office cannot refuse only because the case is pending. It ordered a ten-year passport, but travel still needed the court's prior permission.</li>
</ul>
<p>For jobs, visas and police verification, see <a href="/pending-criminal-case-job-passport-visa/">pending criminal case and job, passport or visa</a>.</p>

<h2>Documents to collect</h2>
<ul>
<li>Passport, visa, and proof of overseas job, residence or study (for NRIs and IT professionals).</li>
<li>FIR copy, chargesheet (if filed) and case number.</li>
<li>Bail or anticipatory bail order, with all conditions.</li>
<li>Any police notice, such as a notice under Section 35 BNSS (old Section 41A CrPC), and proof that you replied or attended.</li>
<li>Any written note or reference given at the airport.</li>
<li>Proposed travel plan, return ticket and overseas address, if seeking court permission.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How long does it take?</h2>
<p>There is no fixed time. It depends on the agency's response, the court's listing and whether the prosecution opposes. An urgent writ may be heard quickly, but nothing is guaranteed. Plan travel only after the order is in hand and communicated to the BoI.</p>

<h2>Mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Ignoring police notices or court dates. This is one of the main grounds for an LOC.</li>
<li>Booking non-refundable tickets before getting court permission.</li>
<li>Assuming a court order automatically updates the airport system.</li>
<li>Hiding a pending case in a passport application.</li>
<li>Travelling against a bail condition.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>LOC against me how to check?</h3>
<p>There is no public online search. If you know there is a case, a lawyer can check the court record and ask the investigating agency.</p>

<h3>Can police issue an LOC just because an FIR is filed?</h3>
<p>Not automatically. An officer of a listed rank must request it. Courts have said it is meant for people evading arrest or the court.</p>

<h3>LOC quash Bombay High Court: is it possible?</h3>
<p>Yes. The Bombay High Court can quash an LOC in a writ petition under Article 226 if it is illegal or unreasonable. You can also first apply to the trial court or to the agency that issued it. Fees depend on the court and stage; see <a href="/criminal-lawyer-fees-pune/">criminal lawyer fees in Pune</a>.</p>

<h3>Bail mila hai, kya main videsh ja sakta hoon?</h3>
<p>Read your bail order first. If it says you need permission to leave India, you must apply to the court before travelling. Going without permission can lead to your bail being cancelled.</p>

<h3>Can I renew my passport if a criminal case is pending?</h3>
<p>Often yes, with the criminal court's permission. If only an FIR is filed and no court has taken cognizance, the 2019 MEA memorandum says Section 6(2)(f) does not apply. Even with a passport, any travel condition in your bail order still applies.</p>

<h3>Can a bank stop me from flying abroad over a loan default?</h3>
<p>In 2024 the Bombay High Court held that public sector bank heads cannot request LOCs. The matter is pending before the Supreme Court. Banks can still seek a travel restraint from a court or the DRT.</p>

<h3>I am an NRI and a 498A case is filed. Will I be stopped?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. It depends on whether an LOC was requested. Matrimonial cruelty is now Section 85 BNS (old Section 498A IPC). Taking legal advice and cooperating with the case early reduces the risk. See <a href="/498a-fir-filed-husband-pune/">498A FIR filed against husband in Pune</a>.</p>

<p><strong>In short:</strong> A complaint of a physical relationship "on the promise of marriage" is now usually filed under Section 69 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS). Sometimes Section 64 BNS (rape, old Section 376 IPC) is added. The key question is whether the promise was false from the very beginning, or was genuine and later broke down. The Supreme Court has repeatedly held that a mere breach of a promise to marry is not, by itself, a crime. The accused can apply for anticipatory bail and, in suitable cases, ask the Bombay High Court to quash the FIR.</p>

<p>This page explains the law for the accused and his family. Genuine victims of deceit exist, and the law protects them. The aim is to help you respond lawfully and calmly.</p>

<h2>Section 69 BNS and Section 64 BNS: what is the difference?</h2>

<p>The BNS replaced the Indian Penal Code (IPC) on 1 July 2024. The IPC had no separate offence for a false promise of marriage. Such cases were filed as rape under Section 376 IPC, on the basis that consent was given under a "misconception of fact" (old Section 90 IPC, now Section 28 BNS).</p>

<p>The BNS created a new, separate offence:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Section 69 BNS (new, no IPC equivalent):</strong> sexual intercourse with a woman "by deceitful means or by making promise to marry to a woman without any intention of fulfilling the same", where it does not amount to rape. "Deceitful means" includes a false promise of a job or promotion, and marrying after hiding one's identity. Punishment: imprisonment up to 10 years, and fine.</li>
<li><strong>Section 64 BNS (old Section 376 IPC):</strong> rape. Punishment: rigorous imprisonment of not less than 10 years, which may extend to life, and fine.</li>
</ul>

<p>Under the First Schedule of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), both offences are cognizable, non-bailable and triable by the Court of Session. Bail is not automatic.</p>

<p>Acts that happened before 1 July 2024 are still dealt with under the IPC. If a relationship spans both periods, the FIR may mix old and new sections.</p>

<h2>The legal test: false from the start, or a promise later broken?</h2>

<p>Courts separate a <strong>false promise</strong> from a <strong>breach of promise</strong>. In a false promise, the man never intended to marry and used the promise to obtain consent. In a breach, he meant it, but things changed later, for example because of family opposition. Only the first can be a crime. The main Supreme Court decisions are:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pramod Suryabhan Pawar v. State of Maharashtra (2019):</strong> two things must be shown. The promise must have been false, given in bad faith, with no intention of keeping it when it was made. And it must have had a direct link to the woman's decision to have a physical relationship. The Court quashed the FIR.</li>
<li><strong>Sonu @ Subhash Kumar v. State of Uttar Pradesh (2021):</strong> there was no allegation that the promise was false at the start, only a later refusal. The Court said "a breach of a promise cannot be said to be a false promise" and quashed the chargesheet.</li>
<li><strong>Kunal Rameshbhai Kalyani v. State of Gujarat (2026 INSC 987):</strong> one of the first Supreme Court rulings on Section 69 BNS itself. The Court held that Section 69 carries the same requirement: the promise must have been made with the intention of never keeping it. The man later refused to marry because his mother did not agree. The Court said this suggested good faith, and quashed the FIR.</li>
</ul>

<p>The law also protects genuine victims. In <strong>Anurag Soni v. State of Chhattisgarh (2019)</strong>, the Supreme Court kept a rape conviction in place, though it reduced the sentence. The promise was false from the start, as the accused's marriage to another woman had already been fixed. The outcome always depends on the facts and the evidence.</p>

<h2>Long consensual relationships</h2>

<p>In <strong>Mahesh Damu Khare v. State of Maharashtra (2024)</strong>, the Supreme Court said the physical relationship must be traceable directly to the false promise. Where a woman knowingly continues a relationship for a long period, it cannot be said with certainty that it was only because of the promise.</p>

<p>In <strong>Shaileshbhai Govindbhai Makwana v. State of Maharashtra (2026)</strong>, an appeal from the Bombay High Court's Aurangabad bench, both parties were already married to others. They stayed in a relationship for over four years. The Supreme Court called it a relationship that soured, not deceit, and quashed the case.</p>

<p>A long relationship is not always a defence. The court will ask what each person knew, what was promised and when, and why the marriage did not happen.</p>

<h2>Anticipatory bail: Section 482 BNSS</h2>

<p>If you fear arrest, you can apply for anticipatory bail under <strong>Section 482 BNSS (old Section 438 CrPC)</strong>. It can be filed in the Pune District and Sessions Court at Shivajinagar, or in the Bombay High Court in Mumbai. There is no Pune bench of the High Court. Read more on <a href="/practice-areas/anticipatory-bail/">anticipatory bail</a> and the <a href="/how-to-get-anticipatory-bail-pune/">step-by-step process in Pune</a>.</p>

<p><strong>Is anticipatory bail barred?</strong> Section 482(4) BNSS excludes accusations under Section 65 BNS (rape of a girl under 16, or under 12) and Section 70(2) BNS (gang rape of a girl under 18). It does <em>not</em> name Section 64 or Section 69. So an application is maintainable in an ordinary Section 64 or 69 case between adults. Whether it is granted depends on the facts.</p>

<p>If the complainant was under 18 at any point in the relationship, the position is very different. The POCSO Act may apply, and the Section 482(4) bar may come in. See <a href="/practice-areas/pocso-defence/">POCSO defence</a>.</p>

<p><strong>Will the complainant be heard?</strong> Section 483(2) BNSS (old Section 439(1A) CrPC) makes the informant's presence compulsory at bail hearings only in Section 65 and 70(2) cases. But in <em>Jagjeet Singh v. Ashish Mishra (2022)</em>, the Supreme Court held that victims have a right to be heard in bail proceedings. So the court may issue notice to the complainant. Courts often impose conditions, such as no contact with her. See <a href="/anticipatory-bail-conditions-pune/">anticipatory bail conditions</a>.</p>

<h2>FIR quashing in the Bombay High Court</h2>

<p>If the FIR does not show a promise that was false from the start, the High Court can quash it. It does so under <strong>Section 528 BNSS (old Section 482 CrPC)</strong> and Article 226 of the Constitution. In Pramod Suryabhan Pawar, Sonu, Kalyani and Makwana, the High Court had refused to quash, and the Supreme Court then quashed the case.</p>

<p>The High Court mainly looks at the FIR and the record, without a full trial. Quashing is not automatic, and disputed facts often go to trial. Read more on <a href="/fir-quashing-bombay-high-court-pune/">FIR quashing in the Bombay High Court</a>.</p>

<h2>What happens next: the usual stages</h2>
<ol>
<li>An FIR is registered. You can <a href="/how-to-check-fir-registered-against-you-maharashtra/">check whether an FIR has been registered</a>.</li>
<li>The complainant's statement is recorded, often also before a Magistrate.</li>
<li>Police may question the accused, seize phones, and arrange medical examinations.</li>
<li>The accused applies for anticipatory bail, or for regular bail after arrest.</li>
<li>Police file a chargesheet. The case goes to the Sessions Court for trial, unless it is quashed or the accused is discharged.</li>
</ol>

<p>How long each stage takes varies widely with the court, the police and the facts.</p>

<h2>Documents and material to collect</h2>
<ul>
<li>A copy of the FIR, or any police notice you have received.</li>
<li>Chats, emails, call records and photos showing how the relationship began and developed, kept in original form.</li>
<li>Material showing why the marriage did not happen, such as family objections.</li>
<li>Proof of each person's marital status, if relevant.</li>
<li>Your identity and address proof, for the bail application.</li>
</ul>

<p>Electronic records usually need a certificate under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (old Section 65B Evidence Act). Do not edit or crop chats. Keep the original phone safe and ask your advocate how to preserve it.</p>

<h2>Mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Do not contact the complainant</strong>, directly or through friends, family or social media. It can look like pressure and can lead to bail being refused or cancelled.</li>
<li><strong>Do not pressure her family</strong> or offer money to withdraw the complaint.</li>
<li><strong>Do not post about the case</strong> or share private photos or chats. That can itself be a serious offence.</li>
<li><strong>Do not delete chats or reset your phone.</strong> Destroying evidence harms your case.</li>
<li><strong>Do not ignore a police notice</strong> or go to the police station without legal advice.</li>
<li><strong>Do not assume the case will simply go away.</strong> See also <a href="/false-fir-pune-legal-remedies/">remedies against a false FIR</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Is Section 69 BNS bailable?</h3>
<p>No. Under the BNSS First Schedule, Section 69 is cognizable, non-bailable and triable by the Court of Session. "Non-bailable" does not mean bail is impossible. It means the court decides, not the police.</p>

<h3>Can I get anticipatory bail in a 376 or Section 69 case?</h3>
<p>Yes, an application can be filed under Section 482 BNSS (old Section 438 CrPC). The bar in Section 482(4) covers only Section 65 and Section 70(2) BNS, which concern girls under 16 or 18. Whether bail is granted depends on the facts.</p>

<h3>Shaadi ka vada karke todna kya crime hai?</h3>
<p>Not by itself. The Supreme Court has held that a genuine promise later broken is not a crime. The offence arises only if the man never intended to marry and used the promise to obtain consent.</p>

<h3>We were in a relationship for years. Can she still file a case?</h3>
<p>Yes, and the police may register an FIR. But in long consensual relationships, courts have often found that the relationship was not based only on a promise of marriage. Each case depends on its facts.</p>

<h3>Can a false promise of marriage FIR be quashed if we compromise?</h3>
<p>Rape and Section 69 are not on the list of offences that can be compounded under Section 359 BNSS (old Section 320 CrPC). A settlement alone does not close the case. The High Court can still quash an FIR if the allegations do not make out the offence.</p>

<h3>What if the girl was under 18?</h3>
<p>Then the law is much stricter. The POCSO Act may apply, consent is not a defence, and anticipatory bail may be barred for some offences. Get legal advice immediately.</p>

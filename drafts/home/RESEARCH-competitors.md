## Method and limits (read first)

- Sites analysed: the four you named, plus two Pune sites found by search (Dhende Associates, Palshikar Legal), plus one extra (Vikram Sawant Inamdar, a Google Sites page) as a low-end contrast. That is seven in all.
- `advocateaashutoshsrivastava.com` works (it redirects to `www.`), so no domain search was needed.
- **Skipped:** `advshirishpatil.in` (Shirish Patil & Associates, Shivajinagar) returned HTTP 502 / a TLS handshake error in the browser and in curl, so I could not capture it. Directories (LawRato, Justdial, RestTheCase, Lead India and similar) were skipped as instructed.
- **"Page 1" is not verified.** The web search tool available to me is US-only and cannot show a Pune SERP. These sites were chosen because they appeared in search results for the two queries, not because I confirmed their position. Treat "ranks on page 1" as unverified.
- **Page weight:** the numbers are decoded response bytes for the first load plus 4 to 8 seconds idle, from Chromium, not network transfer size. After a full scroll the figures are higher (noted where it matters). They are good for comparing sites with each other, not for a Lighthouse score.
- "Rule 36" means Rule 36 of the Bar Council of India Rules (Part VI, Chapter II, Section II, on advertising and solicitation). I flag text that looks like a problem under the usual reading (superlatives, results, testimonials, claims of specialisation, fee or availability claims). This is an editorial flag, not legal advice.
- Years, ratings and review counts below are what each site says. I did not verify any of them.

## Patterns that build trust

1. **A real, well-lit portrait of the advocate, large, in the first screen** (Dongre). It beats every stock photo. Dhende has none and feels like a template as a result.
2. **One plain sentence for a headline**, not a superlative. "Legal help that's clear, honest & on your side." and "Trusted Advocates for Every Legal Challenge" both read as calm. (Note both still use soft self-praise words, "honest" and "Trusted", that we should keep out of our copy.)
3. **Hard, checkable facts as the proof:** a year of enrolment (Dongre "Enrolled 1998 · Bar Council of Maharashtra & Goa"; Dhende "Practising since 1984"), the court names, a street address, a Bar Council number. These are facts, not claims.
4. **Sticky mobile action bar with Call, WhatsApp, and Book** (Dongre). On a phone this is the single most useful trust and conversion device. A pre-filled WhatsApp message ("Hello, I found your website and I would like to speak to an advocate about a legal matter...") lowers the effort further.
5. **A short "how it works" strip** (Consult → Plan → Represent → Resolve, Dongre). It removes fear of the unknown. Keep it process-only, with no promises.
6. **Situation-led entry** ("What do you need help with?", "Not sure where to start?"). Dongre starts it; nobody finishes it for criminal matters.
7. **Light, fast pages.** Dongre (37 requests, 1.8 MB) and Dhende (16 requests, 2.2 MB) feel more premium than the 18 to 32 MB sites, because nothing janks.
8. **Visible Bar Council compliance** done calmly: a footer disclaimer (Dongre) or a one-line note, not a wall.
9. **Dark navy or indigo with gold, serif display type** (Dhende, Prem) reads as "chambers" and is distinct from the cream/green and red/stock-photo looks.
10. **Quiet motion:** count-up numbers, soft fades, a scroll cue. Nothing flashing.

## Patterns to avoid (cheap, risky, or Rule 36 problems)

- **Superlatives in `<title>`, meta, H1 or hero:** "Best", "Top 10", "Leading", "Foremost authority", "Specialist", "Expert" (Upadhye, Srivastava, Prem, Palshikar, Sawant).
- **Success counters and outcomes:** "6500+ Client Success Stories", "Bail Applications Successfully Argued", "Satisfied Clients", "Recent outcomes", "Bail granted" cards, "₹18 lakh recovered", and "Within 15 days they managed to secure bail" (Srivastava, Prem, Dongre, Upadhye).
- **Case studies with real order numbers or identifying detail** (Prem: bail application number, "badminton coach", "Chembur Police station"). Confidentiality problem on top of Rule 36.
- **Testimonials and star ratings in the hero** (Dongre, Upadhye widget, Sawant stars). Also self-marked-up `AggregateRating` in schema (Palshikar).
- **"Free" claims:** "Get Free Experts Advice" (Prem), "Free Legal Tools" (Dhende; probably fine as tools, but the word "Free" next to legal advice is a flag), "Lowest Fees" (Sawant), "affordable rates" (Dhende meta).
- **Availability and speed promises:** "Replies within 2 hours" (Dongre), "same-day or next-day case assessment" (Prem), "Fast-Track Handling of Urgent Arrest" (Prem). Also Dhende's schema (7 days, 09:00 to 21:00) contradicting its visible text (Mon to Sat). No site claimed 24/7 in visible text on its homepage.
- **Award photos as the hero** (Srivastava) and **photos with police officers** (Prem): the first screen should be about the visitor's problem.
- **Disclaimers that contradict the page** (Srivastava says advertising is prohibited, then says "best lawyer in Mumbai").
- **Mandatory consent gates** (Srivastava, Dhende). They stop a frightened visitor before they see a phone number and add a click to the key task. A footer line is enough.
- **Sliders and heavy video** (Prem, Srivastava): 17 to 32 MB, hero text hidden behind slides, no headline on mobile.
- **Rotating "WE ARE ___ LAWYERS"** claiming seven specialisations (Upadhye).
- **Stock gavel, scales or statue photos with pasted SEO text** (Palshikar, Sawant, Upadhye).
- **CTAs that go nowhere:** "Get a Quote" with `href="#"` (Prem), nav parents with `href="#"` (Srivastava), `tel:` link wired to an email (Upadhye), `web.whatsapp.com` links (Palshikar).
- **Wrong section numbers:** Prem's "Section 438 of the BNSS" (it is 482 under BNSS; 438 is the old CrPC number). Dongre's outcome card uses "498A" without the BNS number.
- **Bad structured data:** lowercase "attorney" and `AggregateRating` (Palshikar), no schema at all (Prem, Sawant), generic WebPage only (Upadhye).

## Gaps we can own

1. **Situation-based navigation for criminal matters.** None of the seven organises the first screen by what has happened ("FIR registered against me", "Arrest or remand in a few hours", "Police notice under Section 35 BNSS", "Cheque bounced", "Bail in jail", "Summons from court"). Dongre's "What do you need help with?" is the nearest, but it lists practice areas, not situations, and is mostly family law.
2. **Accurate current section numbers.** Only Upadhye (S.35(3) BNSS), Prem (wrong 438) and Dhende (a stray CrPC/438 mention) touch them. A homepage that correctly says anticipatory bail is BNSS s.482, regular bail by Magistrate s.480 and by the Sessions Court or High Court s.483, and police notice s.35 BNSS, with the old CrPC number shown in smaller text, would be the only one. Verify each against the bare act before publishing; this note comes from my own understanding and from competitor pages that disagree with each other.
3. **Marathi and Hindi.** No site offers a language switch. Prem has Hindi news clips; Dhende has one Marathi block. Our client works in English, Marathi and Hindi. A simple "मराठी / हिंदी" switch with a Marathi H1 and Marathi call-to-action would be unique among Pune criminal sites. (Do a proper Marathi translation by a person, not a machine pass.)
4. **Honest office hours and urgency.** Nobody states, in plain words, what to do at 11 p.m. when someone is arrested. Honest copy such as "Office: Mon to Sat, 10:00 to 19:00. For an urgent arrest, call or WhatsApp; messages are read as soon as possible" is rare and credible. This also fixes the open mismatch between our Google profile and our site noted in `local-seo-context.md`.
5. **Two offices explained clearly.** Dongre and Dhende show one office. We can show Shivajinagar (walk-in, near the Sessions Court) and Pimple Gurav (by appointment) with a map link, a landmark line, and a "which one to visit" line.
6. **Court-first geography.** Nobody pairs the office with a plain explanation of which court hears what in Pune (Magistrate, Sessions, High Court bench matters from Pune, special courts). Dongre does this only in a family-court blog snippet.
7. **A calm, fast page.** Under 1 MB and 20 requests would beat every site here on speed and feel.
8. **Compliant trust without superlatives:** enrolment number and date, court list, languages, address, and plain "what to bring" and "what happens next" content. Only Dongre and Dhende come close.
9. **WhatsApp done right:** a pre-filled WhatsApp message that tells the person what to include (name, police station, date) with the privacy note, rather than "Chat with us".
10. **Correct, complete LegalService / Attorney schema with honest hours and no self-rated stars.** Only Dongre and Dhende are decent here.

## 10 concrete recommendations for our homepage

Constraints used: navy and gold, serif headings, Bar Council Rule 36 compliant, Pune, English with Marathi and Hindi.

1. **Headline that names the situation, not the lawyer.** For example "Arrested, or expecting an FIR? Understand your options before you act." with a sub-line "Advocate Akash R. Chikate, criminal law, Pune. Sessions Court, Magistrate courts and the Bombay High Court." Serif headline (two lines on desktop), navy background, gold underline or gold italic on one phrase only. No "best", "expert", "specialist", "top", "leading", "trusted".
2. **Real portrait in the hero** (tonal, navy backdrop, no cut-out collage, no stock, no AI image) with a caption line made only of facts: "Enrolled with the Bar Council of Maharashtra & Goa, 2020" (use the enrolment number if the client agrees to show it) and "Shivajinagar, Pune".

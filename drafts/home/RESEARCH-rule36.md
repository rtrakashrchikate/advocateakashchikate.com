# Rule 36 research: what an Indian advocate may show on a homepage and hero

Prepared 18 November 2026 for the homepage redesign of Advocate Akash R. Chikate (Pune; enrolled 2020, Bar Council of Maharashtra & Goa, MAH/4681/2020).

This is research and a compliance checklist, not a legal opinion. Where the law is unsettled it says so (section 9). The decision on risk is the advocate's.

How to read the citations: each point carries a source key such as [S1] and a short quote. Keys resolve to URLs in section 11. "Primary" means I read the document itself (the BCI notice, the judgments, the BCI circular and press release). "Secondary" means a news or commentary report, which I flag.

---

## 0. The answer in one paragraph

Rule 36 forbids an advocate from soliciting work or advertising "directly or indirectly". The only website carve-out is the 2008 proviso, which lets an advocate publish the particulars in a short Schedule (name, contact details, enrolment details, qualifications, areas of practice) and nothing else. "Any additional other input in the particulars than approved by the Bar Council of India will be deemed to be violation of Rule 36" [S1]. Since July 2024 the Madras High Court, the BCI (letters of 6 July 2024, press releases of 8 July 2024 and 17 March 2025) and the BCI circular of 17 July 2026 have all tightened the reading. I found no later amendment that widens the Schedule. I also found nothing at all that approves testimonials, ratings, awards, case results, "free consultation", superlatives or urgency calls to action.

## 1. Correction to the research brief: A.K. Balaji (2018) is not an advertising judgment

The brief assumed *Bar Council of India v. A.K. Balaji* (2018) decides what lawyers and law firms may do by way of advertising, websites and directories. I read the full judgment [S8]. It does not.

- It is about whether foreign law firms and lawyers may practise in India. The Supreme Court (13 March 2018, Goel and Lalit JJ.; AIR 2018 SC 1382; (2018) 5 SCC 379) held that they may not practise law in India, but allowed "fly in and fly out" visits and arbitration, subject to BCI rules.
- The words "advertising" and "Rule 36" appear only where the Court records what counsel argued:
  - Para 7 (BCI's submission): "Indian lawyers are prohibited from advertising, canvassing and solicit work but foreign law firms are advertising through websites and canvass and solicit work by assuring results." [S8]
  - Para 31 (BCI's counsel): "Rule 36 in Part VI, Chapter II of the BCI Rules prohibits direct or indirect advertising by advocates, or solicitation by any means whatsoever." The same paragraph records that the US permits some solicitation and that the UK "permits publicity of the law firm but prohibits solicitations". [S8]
- These are recorded arguments, not holdings. The Court did not rule on Rule 36, websites, directories or ratings. The operative ethics holding is para 39: "Ethics of the legal profession apply not only when an advocate appears before the Court. The same also apply to regulate practice outside the Court." [S8]
- The BCI itself uses the case this narrow way. Its 17 March 2025 press release cites it only for jurisdiction: "individuals, associations, firms, companies, juridical persons, and even BPO companies ... fall under the regulatory jurisdiction of the Bar Council of India if, in pith and substance, they engage in the practice of law." [S6] The 17 July 2026 circular says the judgment "records the submission that Rule 36 of the BCI Rules prohibits direct or indirect advertising and solicitation" and concludes "practices prevalent in other jurisdictions cannot be imported into India". [S7, p. 9]

What changed because of Balaji: the practical effect is that ethics rules (Rule 36 included) are said to reach non-litigation and online conduct, and that the BCI can regulate entities that practise law. It did not relax or tighten Rule 36 for an individual advocate's homepage.

The decisions that actually shaped website rules are V.B. Joshi (Supreme Court, 2008, led to the proviso), *Bar Council of Maharashtra v. M.V. Dabholkar* (1976) 2 SCC 291 (the no-advertising principle), the Kerala High Court (2020) and the Madras High Court (3 July 2024). See sections 3 to 6.

## 2. Rule 36 and the 2008 website proviso (exact text)

### 2.1 Rule 36 (Section IV "Duty to Colleagues", Chapter II, Part VI)

> "36. An Advocate shall not solicit work or advertise, either directly or indirectly, whether by circulars, advertisements, touts, personal communications, interviews not warranted by personal relations, furnishing or inspiring newspaper comments or producing his photographs to be published in connection with cases in which he has been engaged or concerned. His sign-board or name-plate should be reasonable size. The sign-board or name-plate or stationery should not indicate that he is or has been associated with any person or organisation or with any particular cause or matter or that he specialises in any particular type of work or that he has been a Judge or an Advocate-General." [S2]

Note on versions: the 2007 Bar Council of Delhi print adds "President or Member of a Bar Council or of any Association" to the sign-board list [S3]. The Indian Kanoon consolidation does not [S2]. The solicitation and "specialises" language is the same in both. Verify the sign-board sentence against the gazetted text before relying on it.

The Indian Kanoon consolidation of the Rules does not contain the 2008 proviso at all (a text search for "website" returns nothing) [S2]. Many lawyers working from consolidated copies therefore miss it. The proviso is in the BCI notice [S1], and the courts quote it [S4, S5].

Rule 36 is enforced as misconduct under Section 35 of the Advocates Act, 1961 [S1, S5 para 30]. The BCI states the consequences as "suspension or cancellation of enrolment, referral to the Supreme Court of India for contempt proceedings, and formal complaints to digital platforms" [S6, p. 3].

### 2.2 The 2008 proviso (BCI Resolution No. 50/2008 of 24 March 2008; notice STBC (Cir.) No. 7/2008 dated 30.4.2008)

> "RESOLVED that the following amendment of Rule 36 in Section IV, Chapter II, Part VI of the Bar Council of India Rules by incorporating a proviso in terms of resolution passed by the Joint Consultative Conference be and is hereby approved."
>
> "PROVIDED that this Rule will not stand in the way of advocates furnishing website information as prescribed in the Schedule under intimation to and as approved by the Bar Council of India. Any additional other input in the particulars than approved by the Bar Council of India will be deemed to be violation of Rule 36 and such advocates are liable to be proceeded with misconduct under Section 35 of the Advocates Act, 1961" [S1]
>
> "The above Amendment has been approved by the Hon'ble Chief Justice of India as provided for under proviso to Section 49 (1) (j) of the Advocates Act, 1961." [S1]

Source quality: [S1] is a PDF print (28 April 2012) of the BCI's own website notice as archived on web.archive.org on 30 December 2008. It is the closest thing to the official text I could reach. The live BCI website (barcouncilofindia.org) renders only through JavaScript and I could not retrieve its rules pages.

### 2.3 The permitted information list (the "Schedule"), exactly as in the BCI notice [S1]

| No. | Item as printed in the Schedule |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Name" |
| 2 | "Address", "Telephone Numbers", "E-mail id" |
| 3(a) | "Enrolment Number" |
| 3(b) | "Date of Enrolment" |
| 3(c) | "Name of State Bar Council where originally enrolled" |
| 3(d) | "Name of State Bar Council on whose roll name stands currently" |
| 3(e) | "Name of the Bar Association of which the Advocate is Member" |
| 4 | "Professional and Academic Qualifications" |
| 5 | "Areas of Practice ( Eg. Civil Criminal Taxation, Labour, etc.)" |
| Declaration | "I hereby declare that the information given is true." (signed with name) |

Three points about this list:

1. There is no line for experience, languages, courts, photographs, ratings, results, awards, clients, hours, or fees. It is a closed list, and the proviso treats additions as a violation.
2. The BCI itself restated the list on 17 July 2026 in almost the same terms: "Rule 36 also permits only limited website information in the prescribed Schedule, including name, address, telephone number, email id, enrolment particulars, Bar Association membership, professional and academic qualifications and broad areas of practice." [S7, p. 7] Note the word "broad".
3. "Under intimation to and as approved by the Bar Council of India" is a procedural condition. The Kerala High Court read it as requiring an application: "the petitioner has to make an application to the Bar Council of India before publishing any website containing information, as indicated in Ext.P1" [S4, para 5]. I found no public BCI portal or form for this. See section 9.

### 2.4 How the 2008 change came about (context)

- The Supreme Court was hearing V.B. Joshi's challenge to Rule 36 (Justices B.N. Agrawal, S.H. Kapadia, D.K. Jain). The Hindu, 30 July 2008, reported "Justice Agrawal told counsel that it would be better if the BCI allowed lawyers to furnish information about their experience and areas of specialisation also" and "Mr. Krishnamani said the BCI would accept this suggestion." [S13, secondary]
- The Schedule as notified on 30 April 2008 does not contain experience or specialisation [S1]. I found no later BCI amendment adding them. In 2014 the BCI chairman said the Council would "pass a resolution to amend Rule 36" to let law firms run websites [S17, secondary]; I found no evidence that it was passed or notified.
- Result: a lawyer who says "the Supreme Court allowed experience on websites" is relying on a hearing report, not on a notified rule.

## 3. The prohibited conduct, in the BCI's and the courts' own words

### 3.1 Rule 36 itself [S2]
"solicit work or advertise, either directly or indirectly, whether by circulars, advertisements, touts, personal communications, interviews not warranted by personal relations, furnishing or inspiring newspaper comments or producing his photographs to be published in connection with cases in which he has been engaged or concerned."
Sign-board, name-plate or stationery must not indicate "that he specialises in any particular type of work".

### 3.2 Madras High Court, *P.N. Vignesh v. Chairman, Bar Council of India*, W.P. No. 31281 of 2019, decided 3 July 2024 (2024:MHC:2515) [S5] (primary; Subramaniam and Kumarappan JJ.)
This case is about listing platforms (Quikr, Sulekha, JustDial), but its reasoning is the clearest judicial statement on ratings, branding and expertise claims:
- Para 14: "self-branding of lawyers and promotions through advertisements, giving customer ratings to the lawyers and unverified claims of expertise in specific areas of law, enlisting their names in business websites and providing offer price for legal consultations. This is clearly unheard of and will have a detrimental effect in the progress of the profession."
- Para 17: gradings "such as Platinum, Top Provider" marked beside each lawyer.
- Para 18: "the websites have also independently provided rating services of lawyers without any basis or authority and the lawyers by enlisting themselves in such websites have brought down the nobility and dignity of the profession."
- Para 29: "The only Proviso allowed for advocates is that they can furnish website information as prescribed in the Schedule under intimation to and as approved by Bar Council of India".
- Para 39: the JustDial listing "is strictly not in consonance with the details allowed to be published by the Bar Council of India under Proviso to Rule 36. There are ratings given against each listed Advocates name. This clearly falls under the ambit of selling lawyers services for a price."
- Para 41: "The Directory services approved by the Bar Council of India is that the names of the Advocates along with the permissible details as mentioned in Rule 36 alone can be published. Any deviation from the said Rule or expanding the scope of the Directory services by parallelly creating a platform of connection for soliciting work between lawyers and litigants are unlawful under the Bar Council of India Rules."
- Para 20 reproduces a discount advertisement ("30% Offer*", "Offer Price", "15% Discount") as the kind of conduct that "degraded the nature of the profession" (para 22).
- Direction (4): platforms must "remove all the contents which are in violation of Rule 36 of BCI Rules" within four weeks. Directions (1) to (3) order the BCI to issue circulars to State Bar Councils to start disciplinary proceedings and to act against intermediaries.
- Para 7: "it is not for any third party to brand or rate the services of a lawyer." Para 8: "Branding culture in the legal profession is detrimental to the society. Ranking or providing customer ratings to lawyers is unheard of and demeans the ethos of the profession." [S5]. Note the wording: the Court's concern is third-party branding and rating, and the lawyer's own listing on those sites; it does not address a lawyer's own homepage (section 9, point 5).

Reach: a High Court judgment binds directly inside its own jurisdiction and is persuasive elsewhere. The BCI has adopted it nationally (3.3). Appeals are pending in the Supreme Court (section 7).

### 3.3 BCI directions after the Madras judgment
- Letter No. BCI:D:3417/2024 dated 6 July 2024 to all State Bar Councils "mandating immediate disciplinary action against advocates engaging in unethical advertising or solicitation of work via online portals" [S6, p. 1, primary]. Reported by LiveLaw on 8 July 2024: the BCI named "illegal solicitation of work" and "providing ratings and offering prices for lawyer services" as the two violations, and said "Strict adherence to Rule 36 of the BCI Rules is mandatory" [S9, secondary].
- Press release dated 17 March 2025 [S6, primary]. It mandates, among other things: "Immediate withdrawal of advertisements violating Rule 36"; "Prohibition against utilizing Bollywood actors, celebrities, or influencers for legal practice promotions"; "Absolute prohibition on the use of social media or digital platforms to directly or indirectly solicit legal work." It also objects to "advocates leveraging religious, cultural, or public events for self-promotion through banners, stalls, and digital advertisements" and warns of "suspension or cancellation of enrollment".

### 3.4 BCI Circular No. BCI:D:4657/2026 dated 17 July 2026 [S7] (primary; I read pp. 1, 5 to 18 of the 37-page scan; pp. 2 to 4 and 19 to 37 are further background and annexed forms, not read)
This is a circular, not an amendment of the Rules. It says it "shall operate as an immediate professional direction under the existing Advocates Act, 1961 and the existing Bar Council of India Rules" and that the BCI "shall separately consolidate and codify these existing obligations ... through the applicable statutory rule-making process under Section 49" [S7, p. 5]. It is aimed at "social media and digital platforms"; the BCI's reasoning is general enough that I treat it as applying to a website.

Directly relevant passages:
- The test (p. 7): "The test is not merely whether the post expressly says 'hire me' or 'contact me'. The test is whether the content, viewed as a whole, uses the Court, Court premises, judicial proceedings, professional work, robe, chamber, client matter, litigation experience or internship to promote oneself, sensationalise legal work, gain followers, create a personal brand, or commercially exploit legal access, or lower the dignity of the institution."
- p. 7: Rule 36 and the Schedule, and the warning that any disclosure "shall not be used as a device for advertisement, claim of superiority, guarantee of results or client solicitation."
- p. 8: claims "such as guaranteed bail, divorce in a few days, sure acquittal, assured stay, instant relief, success formula, special court connection, secret legal trick or any similar expression which suggests certainty of outcome, special influence, shortcut justice or personalised advice to unknown viewers."
- p. 8 (legal-awareness content only): the advocate "should disclose his or her full name, concerned State Bar Council, enrolment number or other verifiable professional identifier, broad area of practice where relevant, and years of standing only where stated accurately. Such disclosure shall not be used as an advertisement, claim of superiority or guarantee of professional competence, but only as a measure of transparency and accountability."
- §14 "Advocates are advised to refrain from" (pp. 15 to 16), selected items:
  - (iv) "using Court buildings, Court names, Court signage, robes, bands, briefs, cause lists, files, client documents or chamber settings as props for personal publicity or social media branding";
  - (v) "publishing content that amounts to direct or indirect advertising, solicitation, client-attraction, projection of special influence, or commercial self-promotion";
  - (i) "using bands, gowns or robes for public display, reels, posts, promotional photographs or social media performance in a manner inconsistent with Rule 5 and Rule 7 of Section I, Chapter II, Part VI";
  - (xiii) "false claims of appearance, false claims of success, fabricated client testimonials or edited material which creates a misleading impression of professional achievement, Court outcome or legal position";
  - (xiv) "using clickbait, fear based marketing or outcome guarantees, including expressions such as guaranteed bail, divorce in a few days, sure acquittal, assured stay, instant relief or similar claims which mislead the public or commodify legal remedies";
  - (xv) "purchasing fake followers, fake engagement, fabricated reviews, fabricated testimonials, bot driven amplification or paid undisclosed promotion to create a false impression of professional credibility, public trust, success rate or standing at the Bar";
  - (xvii) "creating content which states or implies that the creator was personally present in Court, personally argued a matter, personally obtained a relief, or personally handled a client matter when such representation is false, exaggerated, AI generated or otherwise misleading";
  - (xviii) "disclosing case numbers, bail orders, orders in sensitive matters, pleadings, medical papers, identity documents, photographs, contact details, chat records, settlement communications or other client or litigant material in a manner which compromises confidentiality, privacy, privilege, safety, dignity or the administration of justice".
- What is allowed (p. 13, §12): "responsible legal awareness", "academic discussion of judgments", "accurate legal reporting", "public legal education", "neutral case-law updates", and short-form education that is "accurate, contextual, non-soliciting, non-confidential, non-sensational, and does not convert complex legal questions into misleading outcome assurances". Such content "should be accurate, non promotional, non sensational, non soliciting, respectful to Courts and institutions, and should avoid giving outcome guarantees, personalised legal advice to unknown viewers, or exaggerated representations of professional standing" (p. 14) and should "carry a clear disclaimer that viewers must seek independent professional advice on their own facts" (p. 14).
- Limit on reach (p. 17): "it does not seek to police ordinary personal life, dress, private expression or lawful personal social media use". The concern is personal content "deliberately linked to professional identity".
- Enrolled advocates need not file a new affidavit; the BCI asked State Bar Councils to sensitise them (reported by LiveLaw and Bar & Bench; the affidavit and undertaking requirement is for new enrolment, students and interns) [S16, secondary].

### 3.5 Bar Council of Maharashtra & Goa (the advocate's own regulator)
- Letter of 21 September 2019 (Secretary Pravin Ranpise) to all bar associations, subject "Prohibition of making advertisement in the newspaper/WhatsApp by advocates": "All Advocates in State of Maharashtra & Goa are hereby prohibited from making any kind of advertisement." It cites "Rule 36 Section IV Part VI of Chapter II" and warns "immediate action shall be taken by the State Bar Council regarding misconduct." [S10, secondary report of the letter]
- I did not find any BCMG circular, order or Bombay High Court judgment specifically about advocates' own websites, rankings, reviews or listing sites. The BCMG and the Bombay High Court are therefore bound by, and in practice will apply, the BCI position above.
- Practical point: the BCMG is the body that would receive a complaint about this website. Its 2019 letter reads Rule 36 as a flat prohibition.

### 3.6 Kerala High Court, *Biju Gopal v. Bar Council of Kerala*, WP(C) 12968/2010, 9 March 2020 [S4] (primary)
An advocate was put to disciplinary inquiry for a "family lawyers" website. The Court noted the 2008 proviso and Resolution 50/2008, held that "the petitioner has to make an application to the Bar Council of India before publishing any website containing information, as indicated", and gave a month to apply, deferring the Kerala inquiry until the BCI decided. The BCI was to decide within six months. It illustrates that (a) disciplinary action over websites is real, and (b) the BCI application route is the safe harbour.

## 6. Homepage element table

Legend: ALLOWED = within the Schedule or clearly consistent with it. RISKY = not in the Schedule and no authority either way, or depends on wording; decide knowingly. NOT ALLOWED = contradicted by Rule 36, the Schedule's closed list, or BCI and court statements.

Keys: [Sched] = [S1]; [R36] = [S2]; [MHC] = [S5]; [Circ] = [S7].

| Homepage element | Verdict | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portrait photo | RISKY (low) | Not in the Schedule, which is a closed list. Rule 36 text targets photographs "in connection with cases". No BCI or court order found against a plain portrait. If used: no robes, bands, court backdrop. | [Sched] closed list; [R36] "photographs to be published in connection with cases"; [Circ p. 15 (i)] |
| Name, enrolment number, enrolment date, State Bar Council | ALLOWED | Schedule items 1 and 3(a) to 3(d). Use the exact date from the enrolment certificate. | [Sched] "Enrolment Number", "Date of Enrolment", "Name of State Bar Council" |
| Bar Association membership | ALLOWED | Schedule item 3(e), if he is a member. | [Sched] |
| "Years of experience" statement (current site says "8+ years") | NOT ALLOWED as a claim; also factually inconsistent | Not in the Schedule; circular treats litigation experience used to promote oneself as indirect advertising. Enrolment in 2020 gives about six years in November 2026, so "8+ years" must be corrected or removed whatever Rule 36 says. State the enrolment date instead and let readers count. | [Sched]; [Circ pp. 7, 8, 16 (xiii) false claims] |
| "Enrolled 2020" | ALLOWED | It is the date of enrolment (item 3(b)). Prefer the full date. | [Sched] |
| Languages spoken | RISKY (low) | Not in the Schedule. Factual and non-comparative; no authority against it. | [Sched] closed list |
| Courts practised before | RISKY | Not in the Schedule. A truthful factual list is not specifically condemned; false or inflated appearance claims are. Safer: use the office address ("near the Pune District and Sessions Court") as an address, not as a credential. | [Sched]; [Circ p. 16 (xiii), (xvii)] |
| Qualifications (B.A. LL.B., LL.M.) | ALLOWED | "Professional and Academic Qualifications". | [Sched] item 4 |
| Practice-area list | ALLOWED if broad; RISKY if granular or labelled "specialist" | Schedule item 5 gives "Civil Criminal Taxation, Labour, etc."; the BCI restated "broad areas of practice". "Specialises" is barred on signs and stationery. A long menu of sub-niches reads as specialisation claims. | [Sched] item 5; [Circ p. 7]; [R36] |
| Office address | ALLOWED | Schedule item 2. | [Sched] |
| Office hours | RISKY (low) | Not listed; reasonably an adjunct of "Address" and "Telephone". Facts only; no "open 24/7". | [Sched] item 2 |
| Phone, email | ALLOWED | Schedule item 2. | [Sched] |
| WhatsApp number or button | RISKY (low) | A number is a telephone particular. A prominent "Chat now" push, or any WhatsApp outreach or broadcast, is solicitation territory; the BCMG letter targets WhatsApp advertising. Keep the label neutral ("WhatsApp: number"). | [Sched]; [BCMG, S10] |
| Consultation or contact form | RISKY (low) | Not in the Schedule; an inbound channel is not itself an advertisement, but any hook ("get a call back in 30 minutes", "free") makes it solicitation. Keep it a neutral "Contact the office" form. | [MHC paras 16, 17, 40] |

| Case results or reported orders (as "my wins") | NOT ALLOWED | Success claims, "personally obtained a relief", and disclosure of orders and client material are all listed as misconduct risks. | [Circ p. 16 (xiii), (xvii), (xviii)] |

| General explainer on a judgment or statute | ALLOWED with conditions | Circular encourages "neutral case-law updates" and "public legal education" if non-promotional, non-soliciting, accurate, with a disclaimer. | [Circ pp. 13, 14] |
| Awards | NOT ALLOWED | Outside Schedule; claims of superiority and standing are improper. Current site has "Awards & Recognition" and "Award Winning". | [Sched]; [Circ p. 7, p. 16 (xv)] |
| Media mentions | NOT ALLOWED (strict); RISKY if unsolicited and neutral | Rule 36 bars "furnishing or inspiring newspaper comments" and "interviews not warranted by personal relations". Not in Schedule. | [R36]; [Sched] |
| Blog and guides | ALLOWED with conditions | See "General explainer". Author block limited to Schedule particulars; no case results; disclaimer. | [Circ pp. 8, 13, 14] |
| Fees (amounts, packages, discounts) | NOT ALLOWED | Outside the Schedule; the Madras HC treated posted prices and offers as selling legal services. A neutral line that fees are discussed at consultation is lower risk (RISKY (low)). Rules 20 and 38 also regulate fees. | [MHC paras 15, 19 to 22]; [S8 para 32 on Rule 20] |
| "Best", "top", "leading", "expert", "specialist", "No. 1" | NOT ALLOWED | Comparative or expertise claims; "Top Provider" grades and "unverified claims of expertise" condemned; "specialises" barred. Current site has "Expert", "Specialist", "Best Defence". | [MHC paras 14, 17]; [R36]; [Circ p. 7] |
| "Dedicated" | RISKY (low) | Not a superiority or result claim, but a quality claim outside the Schedule. Prefer describing what the office does. | [Sched]; [Circ p. 7] |
| "Relentless", "fight for you", "I fight for your rights" | RISKY (high); avoid | Emotive outcome and zeal marketing; the BCI objects to "commercial self-promotion" and content that implies results or special influence. No text bans these words by name. | [Circ p. 15 (v), p. 8] |
| Urgency lines ("24/7", "call now", "book today") | NOT ALLOWED | Direct solicitation; "instant relief", "shortcut justice" listed. | [R36]; [Circ p. 8, p. 16 (xiv)] |
| Disclaimer banner | ALLOWED; recommended | The Schedule carries a truth declaration; the circular asks educational content to carry a "clear disclaimer". A banner does not cure prohibited content. | [Sched] "I hereby declare that the information given is true."; [Circ p. 14] |

## 7. How the 2018 decision and later BCI positions changed or clarified the rule

1. **2008:** the only relaxation. A closed Schedule, subject to intimation and approval [S1].
2. **2018 (Balaji):** no change to Rule 36 for individual advocates. It confirmed ethics rules reach non-litigation conduct (para 39) and recorded BCI's arguments that Rule 36 is a flat ban [S8].
3. **2019 (BCMG):** local reaffirmation: "prohibited from making any kind of advertisement" [S10].
4. **2020 (Kerala):** websites are lawful only after application to the BCI [S4].
5. **2024 (Madras HC, then BCI):** ratings, gradings, price offers and "expertise" labels are Rule 36 violations; BCI turns this into disciplinary directions to every State Bar Council [S5, S6].
6. **2025 (BCI):** extends to social media, influencers and celebrity endorsement; "absolute prohibition" on digital solicitation [S6].
7. **2026 (BCI circular):** the first BCI text that explicitly tests *content as a whole* ("The test is not merely whether the post expressly says 'hire me'") and names outcome claims, success claims, fabricated reviews and testimonials, and comparative superiority. It restates the Schedule as "broad areas of practice" and says disclosures are not to be used as "claim of superiority or guarantee of professional competence" [S7].

Direction of travel: strictly more restrictive. Nothing found loosens the 2008 Schedule.

## 8. Ten safest wording patterns for a hero headline and sub-line

All use only Schedule particulars, general legal information, or neutral process statements. Bracketed items to be filled with verified facts. None promises, compares or urges.

| # | Headline | Sub-line | Why it holds up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Advocate Akash R. Chikate | Criminal law practice in Pune. B.A. LL.B., LL.M. Enrolled with the Bar Council of Maharashtra & Goa, MAH/4681/2020. | Pure Schedule items 1, 3, 4, 5. Confidence comes from plainness and verifiability. |
| 2 | Criminal law, Pune | Advocate Akash R. Chikate. Areas of practice: criminal law, cyber law, family law. | Broad area labels exactly as the Schedule's "Civil Criminal Taxation, Labour". No "specialist". |
| 3 | A criminal law practice at Shivajinagar, Pune | Office near the Pune District and Sessions Court. Phone [number]. Email [address]. | Address and phone are Schedule item 2. The court proximity is an address fact. |
| 4 | Plain-English guides to criminal procedure in Maharashtra | Written by Advocate Akash R. Chikate. General information, not legal advice. | Falls in the circular's approved "public legal education" and carries the disclaimer it asks for. |
| 5 | Know the stages before you decide | A short guide to FIR, bail, charge-sheet and trial under the BNSS. General information by Advocate Akash R. Chikate. | Educational, non-soliciting, no outcome language. |
| 6 | Every matter is read on its own facts | Advocate Akash R. Chikate, Pune. Criminal law. | A statement of method, not a result. Avoid adding "personal attention" or "dedicated". |
| 7 | Advocate. Criminal law. Pune. | Marathi, Hindi, English. Enrolled [full date], Bar Council of Maharashtra & Goa. | Short and assured. Languages are the one low-risk addition beyond the Schedule (section 9). |
| 8 | Practising under the Advocates Act, 1961 and the Bar Council of India Rules | Advocate Akash R. Chikate. Enrolment no. MAH/4681/2020. | Confidence through accountability; checkable by anyone. |
| 9 | If you are reading this after a police notice or an arrest, start with the procedure | A plain guide to your rights and the next steps. General information, not advice, from Advocate Akash R. Chikate. | Empathetic and useful. Avoid "free", "call now", "24/7", "guaranteed bail". Check the "after a police notice" framing is not read as fear-based marketing; if in doubt use pattern 5. |
| 10 | Law explained by an advocate you can identify | Name, enrolment number, State Bar Council and office address are on every page. | Transparency as the selling point, which mirrors the circular's "measure of transparency and accountability". Drop the headline if "you can identify" reads as a comparative claim. |

Do not use: "best", "top", "leading", "expert", "specialist", "trusted", "dedicated", "relentless", "fight for you", "free consultation", "no obligation", "24/7", "call now", "guaranteed", "100%", "8+ years", "award", star ratings, "testimonials", counters of cases or clients.

Layout cautions that follow from the sources: a calm portrait without robes or bands; no court-premises backgrounds; one plain contact block; the disclaimer visible without scrolling on the first screen.

## 9. Where the law is unsettled (advocate's call)

1. **Is the Schedule the only thing a website may contain?** The proviso reads "Any additional other input in the particulars than approved". The BCI's 2026 statement is "permits only limited website information in the prescribed Schedule". Strict reading: closed list, so languages, a portrait, office hours and a contact form are all technically extra. Many Indian advocate sites carry more, and enforcement I could find has targeted platforms, influencers, and Rule 36 complaints. A neutral extra is lower risk than a promotional one; I found no disciplinary decision on a personal homepage that carries only neutral extras.
2. **Intimation and approval mechanism.** The proviso says "under intimation to and as approved by the Bar Council of India". The Kerala HC required an application [S4]. I found no public BCI portal or form. Whether silence amounts to approval is unclear. Safer practice: send the BCI (and copy the BCMG) a written intimation with the signed Schedule and declaration, and keep proof of delivery. I recommend the advocate or a colleague confirm the current procedure with the BCI office.
3. **Experience and specialisation.** The Supreme Court Bench suggested them in 2008 and BCI counsel agreed [S13], but the notified Schedule omits them [S1] and no later amendment was found. "Specialises" remains barred on signs [S2]. Treat the 2008 hearing as history, not as permission.
4. **Portrait photograph.** The Rule bars photographs "in connection with cases". Whether a plain portrait is an extra "input" is untested.
5. **Google reviews and rating stars.** No court or BCI statement addresses an advocate's own embedded rating, or a Google Business Profile with reviews he did not post. The nearest authority (Madras HC, BCI notices) is against ratings on third-party sites [S5]. Under the Madras HC reasoning even a listing site may publish only the Schedule particulars, so a Google Business Profile that shows only Schedule details is lowest risk; soliciting or embedding reviews is the higher-risk step.
6. **Does the July 2026 circular reach a website?** Its text speaks of "social media and digital platforms". The advocate's own homepage is a digital platform. It is also a circular, not a notified Rule, and the BCI says it will later codify it under Section 49 [S7, p. 5].
7. **Constitutional validity.** Commercial speech is protected under Article 19(1)(a) (*Tata Press v. MTNL* (1995) 5 SCC 139, cited in [S14, S19]; I did not read the judgment). No Supreme Court decision I found strikes down or narrows Rule 36. Pending matters could move the law in either direction: Sulekha's and JustDial's SLPs against the Madras HC order (notice issued 13 Nov 2024) [S11] and *Anil Pandey v. BCI* W.P.(C) 817/2026 (notice 14 July 2026, returnable 15 September 2026) [S12]. I found no later order.
8. **Disclaimers.** The notified requirement is the signed truth declaration. A website disclaimer is customary and the circular asks for one on educational content, but a disclaimer does not make a prohibited element permissible.
9. **Legal-awareness content versus advertising.** Permitted by the circular, but "viewed as a whole" [S7 p. 7]. A homepage built mostly from calls to action around "educational" content could still be read as advertising.
10. **Rule 36 wording discrepancy.** The sign-board clause differs between the 2007 Delhi print and the Indian Kanoon consolidation [S2, S3]. Check the gazetted text.

<p><strong>In short:</strong> To meet someone at Yerawada Central Prison, Pune, the prisoner must first give the jail your name as a visitor. Families can then visit in person or book a video meeting (e-Mulakat) through the ePrisons portal linked on the Maharashtra Prisons Department website. After bail is granted, the person is released only when the bail bond and surety are accepted by the court and a release order reaches the jail. Visit rules change often, so always check with the jail before you travel.</p>

<h2>First, confirm where the person is held</h2>
<p>When police arrest someone, they must tell a relative or friend about the arrest and where the person is held. This duty is in Section 48 BNSS (old Section 50A CrPC).</p>
<p>At the first hearing, the Magistrate orders police custody or judicial custody (jail). Our page on <a href="/police-kothadi-nyayalayin-kothadi-farak/">police custody vs judicial custody</a> explains the difference.</p>
<p>To confirm which jail, use these routes:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ask the investigating officer at the police station for the remand details.</li>
<li>Ask the lawyer who appeared at the remand hearing. The remand order names the custody.</li>
</ul>
<p>Please use these channels only for your own family member or client. Do not search for other people's prisoners out of curiosity.</p>

<h2>How to find the next court date</h2>
<p>The eCourts Services website (services.ecourts.gov.in) and the eCourts mobile app show the status of district court cases. You can search by CNR number, case number, or FIR number and police station.</p>
<p>The case page shows the next hearing date. The CNR is a 16-character code on court papers; ask the lawyer for it.</p>

<h2>How to meet a prisoner at Yerwada (mulakat)</h2>
<p>The Maharashtra Prisons Department publishes a Citizen's Charter on mahaprisons.gov.in. It sets out the basic visit rules:</p>
<ul>
<li>After admission, the prisoner must give jail officials the names of the relatives who will visit.</li>
<li>Visits are free of charge.</li>
<li>Nothing can be handed over or taken during the visit.</li>
<li>The jail Superintendent fixes the timings for family and lawyer visits.</li>
</ul>
<p>The Charter on the website is an older edition. It mentions one visit a week for undertrials, usually about 20 minutes, with up to five family members or two friends at a time. Practice may have changed since, so confirm current days, timings and numbers with the jail.</p>
<p>News reports from 2025 describe waits of several hours outside Yerawada on visit days. Go early and carry water.</p>

<h2>e-Mulakat: booking a video meeting</h2>
<p>Maharashtra started e-Mulakat (video meetings) in all its prisons in July 2023 under the ePrisons system. The Maharashtra Prisons Department website links to "Emulakat for Prisoners and Lawyers" on the ePrisons National Prisons Information Portal (eprisons.nic.in).</p>
<p>News reports cite a March 2024 circular. It allows undertrial prisoners four e-Mulakats a month and convicted prisoners two a month, subject to the rules. Only relatives, friends and lawyers listed by the prisoner are allowed.</p>
<p>The registration form generally asks for:</p>
<ol>
<li>Your name, address, age and relation to the prisoner.</li>
<li>An identity proof type and its number, with your mobile number and email.</li>
<li>The state, the prison, the prisoner's details, and whether you want a physical or video visit.</li>
<li>An OTP sent to your mobile or email to confirm the request.</li>
</ol>
<p>The jail approves or rejects the request. If approved, you receive the date, time and joining details. Prison staff supervise sessions.</p>

<h3>Phone calls from the prisoner</h3>
<p>Yerawada began a smart-card calling facility in June 2023. At launch, each prisoner had to register three phone numbers in advance. Reports then described three calls a month of up to 10 minutes each, with some categories of prisoners excluded.</p>

<h2>What ID to carry and what not to take</h2>
<p>Carry an original government photo ID, ideally the same one you used for registration. Aadhaar, voter ID, driving licence and passport are the usual choices.</p>
<p>The Charter lists items that prisoners may not keep. These include mobile phones, cash, gold or silver valuables, alcohol, drugs, weapons and sharp objects. Do not carry them to the visit.</p>

<h2>Sending money or items</h2>
<p>Prisoners buy daily items from the jail canteen using money held in their Prisoner's Private Cash (PPC) account. Do not try to hand cash during a visit.</p>
<p>A 2023 report said families can send money by money order to the jail or by online transfer to the PPC account. The same report said the Additional Director General (Prisons) raised the monthly money-order limit to Rs 10,000 in October 2023. Ask the jail's office for the current limit and method before sending.</p>
<p>For clothes, books or food, ask the jail what is allowed first.</p>

<h2>Release after bail: how it actually works</h2>
<p>Release is not automatic on the day bail is granted. There are several steps.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Bail order.</strong> The court passes the order and fixes the bond amount, surety and conditions.</li>
<li><strong>Bail bond and surety.</strong> The accused's bond and the surety's bond are filed in the court named in the bail order.</li>
<li><strong>Surety verification.</strong> The court checks the surety's identity, address and means. Sometimes it asks the police to verify.</li>
<li><strong>Release order.</strong> Once the bonds are accepted, the court sends a release order to the jail. This is required by Section 487 BNSS (old Section 442 CrPC).</li>
<li><strong>Jail check and release.</strong> The jail confirms the person is not wanted in another case, then releases them.</li>
</ol>
<p>If the person is in custody in another case, they stay in jail for that case. If the release order reaches the jail late in the day, release may move to the next day.</p>

<h3>What the Supreme Court has said about delays</h3>
<p>In <em>In Re: Policy Strategy for Grant of Bail</em> (order dated 31 January 2023), the Supreme Court gave directions to stop delays:</p>
<ul>
<li>The court granting bail emails a soft copy of the order to the jail Superintendent for the prisoner.</li>
<li>If the person is not released within 7 days of bail, the jail must inform the Secretary of the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA).</li>
<li>If bail bonds are not furnished within one month, the court may itself consider changing the conditions.</li>
<li>Courts may avoid insisting on a local surety where that causes delay.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you cannot find a surety, the lawyer can ask the court for a cash deposit instead. Section 490 BNSS (old Section 445 CrPC) allows this, at the court's discretion.</p>

<h2>Which court handles bail</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Magistrate court:</strong> bail in most cases at the start, under Section 478 BNSS (old 436 CrPC) for bailable offences and Section 480 BNSS (old 437 CrPC) for non-bailable ones. Pune and Pimpri-Chinchwad have Magistrate courts.</li>
<li><strong>Sessions Court, Shivajinagar:</strong> bail in serious cases, or after the Magistrate refuses bail, under Section 483 BNSS (old 439 CrPC).</li>
<li><strong>Bombay High Court, Mumbai:</strong> if the Sessions Court refuses bail. There is no Pune bench, so the matter is heard in Mumbai. See <a href="/bail-rejected-sessions-court-pune-what-next/">bail rejected by the Sessions Court</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Documents to keep ready</h2>
<ul>
<li>FIR copy, arrest memo and remand order.</li>
<li>Case number or CNR number, and the court name.</li>
<li>Copy of the bail order once passed.</li>
<li>The surety's photo ID, address proof and photographs.</li>
<li>Proof of the surety's means, such as property papers or salary proof, as the court asks.</li>
</ul>
<p>Our page on <a href="/jamanat-kitne-din-me-milti-hai-kharcha-documents-pune/">bail time, cost and documents</a> lists surety papers in more detail.</p>

<h2>Realistic time</h2>
<p>Release can happen on the day the bonds are accepted. It can take several days if surety verification is slow or papers are missing. The Supreme Court's 7-day and one-month marks show what is treated as too long.</p>

<h2>When to approach a lawyer</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bail:</strong> as soon as the person is sent to judicial custody.</li>
<li><strong>Default bail:</strong> if police do not file the chargesheet within 60 or 90 days, depending on the offence, the accused can claim bail under Section 187(3) BNSS (old Section 167(2) CrPC). The application must be made before the chargesheet is filed. See <a href="/practice-areas/default-bail-undertrial-release/">default bail and undertrial release</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Long detention:</strong> Section 479 BNSS (old Section 436A CrPC) allows release after a set part of the maximum sentence. Exceptions apply.</li>
<li><strong>Bail granted but no release:</strong> if surety or bond problems hold up release.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the family cannot afford a lawyer, a person in custody is entitled to free legal services under Section 12(g) of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. Section 341 BNSS (old Section 304 CrPC) also provides a lawyer at State expense in Sessions trials where the accused cannot afford one. Read our <a href="/legal-aid-india-free-legal-help-maharashtra/">legal aid guide</a>. For private representation, fees depend on court and stage; see <a href="/criminal-lawyer-fees-pune/">criminal lawyer fees in Pune</a>.</p>

<h2>Mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Travelling for a visit without confirming that your name is on the prisoner's visitor list.</li>
<li>Paying "agents" outside the jail who promise faster mulakat or release. Use official channels only.</li>
<li>Carrying cash, phones or food into the visit area without checking the rules.</li>
<li>Assuming release is automatic once bail is granted. Follow up on the bond and release order.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Yerwada jail mulakat kaise kare?</h3>
<p>First, the prisoner must give the jail your name as a visitor. Then you can visit in person on the permitted days, or register for e-Mulakat on the ePrisons portal. Carry original photo ID and check timings with the jail before going.</p>

<h3>How do I book e-Mulakat for Yerwada jail?</h3>
<p>Use the "Emulakat for Prisoners and Lawyers" link on mahaprisons.gov.in, which opens the ePrisons visitor registration form. Fill in your details, ID, the prison and prisoner details, and choose a video visit. The jail approves the request and sends the date, time and joining details.</p>

<h3>How many e-Mulakats are allowed per month?</h3>
<p>News reports of a March 2024 circular say undertrials get four e-Mulakats a month and convicts two. The rules can change, so confirm the current limit on the portal or with the jail.</p>

<h3>Jail se release kaise hota hai after bail?</h3>
<p>The bail bond and surety are filed and accepted in court. The court then sends a release order to the jail. The jail checks that the person is not needed in another case, and then releases them.</p>

<h3>Bail mil gayi, phir bhi release kyon nahi hua?</h3>
<p>Common reasons are a pending surety verification, missing documents, or custody in another case. A release order reaching the jail late can also push release to the next day. If release does not happen within 7 days, the jail must inform the District Legal Services Authority.</p>

<h3>How do I find a prisoner's next court date?</h3>
<p>Search on eCourts Services or the eCourts app using the CNR number, case number, or FIR number with the police station. The case page shows the next hearing date. The lawyer can also confirm it.</p>

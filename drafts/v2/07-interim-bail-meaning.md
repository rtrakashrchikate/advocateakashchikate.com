<p><strong>In short:</strong> Interim bail is temporary bail. A court grants it for a short period, most often while it decides the main bail application, or for a specific reason such as illness or a death in the family. It is not the same as regular bail, which lasts until the case moves on. It is also different from parole and furlough, which are granted to convicted prisoners by the prison authorities. This guide is for families dealing with the Pune courts and the Bombay High Court in Mumbai.</p>

<h2>What interim bail means</h2>
<p>"Interim" means for the time being. An interim bail order releases the accused now and lists the matter again, so the court can hear the main application properly. The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) and the old CrPC do not define "interim bail". Courts grant it as part of their power to grant bail.</p>
<p>The Supreme Court said in <em>Sukhwant Singh v. State of Punjab</em> (2009) 7 SCC 559 that a court hearing a regular bail application has the power to grant interim bail until the application is finally decided. The Court linked this to Article 21, the right to life and personal liberty. The power is discretionary: a court may grant it or refuse it.</p>

<h2>When courts may grant interim bail</h2>
<p>There is no fixed list. A court may consider interim bail when the main application cannot be decided at once, or when there is a pressing need. Common situations are:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pending hearing.</strong> The court needs time, for example to read the case papers or hear the Public Prosecutor, and it releases the accused for a short period.</li>
<li><strong>Medical grounds.</strong> The accused, or a close family member, is seriously ill. Section 480(1) BNSS expressly allows a court to grant bail to a sick or infirm person even where the usual bar for serious offences applies.</li>
<li><strong>Family grounds.</strong> A death in the family, or another urgent family event. The court decides whether the reason is genuine and how long is reasonable.</li>
</ul>
<p>Medical papers help. A report from a treating doctor or hospital, with the diagnosis and the advised treatment, shows the court what is needed. The court may ask the jail doctor or a government hospital for its own report.</p>

<h2>Interim anticipatory bail</h2>
<p>Anticipatory bail is sought before arrest under Section 482 BNSS (old Section 438 CrPC). While the application is pending, a court may protect the applicant from arrest until it decides. This is often called interim anticipatory bail or interim protection. It lasts only until the court decides the application, or until the date the order gives.</p>
<ul>
<li>If the court grants anticipatory bail, the protection continues under the final order. The Supreme Court has held that anticipatory bail is not ordinarily limited to a fixed period (<em>Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi)</em>, decided 29 January 2020; and <em>Siddharam Satlingappa Mhetre v. State of Maharashtra</em>, (2011) 1 SCC 694). Check the exact words of your order.</li>
<li>If the court refuses, the interim protection ends. The police can then arrest, subject to the law.</li>
</ul>
<p>Read <a href="/how-to-get-anticipatory-bail-pune/">how to get anticipatory bail in Pune</a> and <a href="/practice-areas/anticipatory-bail/">anticipatory bail</a>. Section 482 BNSS does not apply to offences under Section 65 and 70(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita.</p>

<h2>Parole and furlough are different</h2>
<p>People often mix these up with interim bail. They are not bail.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Interim bail</strong> is granted by a court to an accused who is under trial or under investigation.</li>
<li><strong>Parole</strong> is temporary release of a convicted prisoner for a specific reason, such as serious illness in the family.</li>
<li><strong>Furlough</strong> is a temporary release of a convicted prisoner, as a matter of prison policy, after serving part of the sentence.</li>
</ul>
<p>In Maharashtra, parole and furlough are governed by the Maharashtra Prisons (Furlough and Parole) Rules, 2024, notified on 2 December 2024. They replaced the 1959 rules and were made under the Prisons Act, 1894. Under the rules, the prison department decides the application, and an appeal lies within the department. The rules also require a surety and bond. The detailed periods, eligibility and exclusions are in the rules, and parts of them have been challenged in the Bombay High Court. Read the current text, or ask the prison office, before relying on any number.</p>
<p>If your relative is an undertrial in custody, parole and furlough are not the usual route. The route is a bail application to the court. See also <a href="/practice-areas/police-custody-remand/">police custody and remand</a> for the stages before bail.</p>

<h2>Duration and extension</h2>
<p>The law fixes no standard period. The order says how long the bail lasts. It may run until the next date of hearing, or for a set number of days. A court may extend it if you apply before it ends and show a reason. It may also refuse. The Supreme Court has said that bail applications should ordinarily be decided within two weeks unless the law requires otherwise (<em>Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI</em>, (2022) 10 SCC 51). This is a guideline, so the actual time depends on the court and its list.</p>
<ul>
<li>Note the date and time to surrender or appear in the order.</li>
<li>If more time is needed, apply for an extension before the date, with documents.</li>
<li>Follow every condition in the bond.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What happens when interim bail ends</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>The main application is allowed.</strong> The accused is released on regular bail, on a bond and surety, as the court directs.</li>
<li><strong>The main application is rejected.</strong> The interim order ends, and the accused is taken or surrenders into custody, unless a higher court protects them. The next step is the Sessions Court after a Magistrate, or the Bombay High Court after the Sessions Court. See <a href="/bail-rejected-sessions-court-pune-what-next/">bail rejected: what next</a>.</li>
<li><strong>The accused does not surrender or appear.</strong> The bond can be forfeited under Section 491 BNSS, and the court can order arrest. Under Section 480(5) and 483(3) BNSS, a court can order a person released on bail to be arrested and committed to custody.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Treating interim bail as final. It ends on the date in the order.</li>
<li>Asking for an extension after the date has passed.</li>
<li>Applying for parole or furlough when the person has not been convicted.</li>
<li>Giving medical grounds without a doctor's paper.</li>
<li>Ignoring conditions, such as attending court or the police station.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Interim bail kya hota hai?</h3>
<p>Interim bail is temporary jamanat. The court releases the accused for a short time, usually while the main bail application is pending, or for a reason like illness. It ends on the date in the order.</p>
<h3>What is the difference between interim bail and regular bail?</h3>
<p>Interim bail is short and temporary. Regular bail is granted after the court decides the main application and continues under conditions. Regular bail comes under Section 480 or 483 BNSS (old Sections 437 and 439 CrPC).</p>
<h3>What is interim anticipatory bail?</h3>
<p>It is protection from arrest given before the court decides an anticipatory bail application. It lasts until the application is decided or the date stated in the order.</p>
<h3>Can I get interim bail on medical grounds?</h3>
<p>A court can consider it. You need medical papers, and the court decides how long. Section 480(1) BNSS allows bail to a sick or infirm person in cases where a bar would otherwise apply.</p>
<h3>Is interim bail the same as parole?</h3>
<p>No. Interim bail is granted by a court to an accused. Parole is temporary release of a convicted prisoner, decided under the Maharashtra Prisons (Furlough and Parole) Rules, 2024.</p>
<h3>Can interim bail be extended?</h3>
<p>Yes, if the court allows it. Apply before the order expires and give a reason. The court may extend, change or refuse.</p>
<h3>What happens if I do not surrender when interim bail ends?</h3>
<p>The court can forfeit the bond and order arrest. It can also reject later bail requests more strictly. Surrender on the date, or apply for an extension before it.</p>

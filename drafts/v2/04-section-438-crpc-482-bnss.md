<p><strong>In short:</strong> Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) was the anticipatory bail section. Since 1 July 2024, the same provision is Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS). It lets a person who fears arrest for a non-bailable offence ask the High Court or Sessions Court to order that, if arrested, he must be released on bail.</p>

<h2>Section 438 CrPC is now Section 482 BNSS</h2>
<p>The BNSS replaced the CrPC from 1 July 2024. The old Section 438 CrPC is the new Section 482 BNSS. Many people still search for "section 438 CrPC" or "anticipatory bail section in CrPC", and the old number still appears in older judgments and in FIRs from before that date.</p>
<p>The wording of the two sections is almost the same. The changes are in cross-references, such as the numbers of the sections on bail conditions, and in one exception for certain sexual offences. Both are shown in the table below.</p>

<h2>What Section 482 BNSS (old 438 CrPC) says</h2>
<p>This is a close summary of the text. It is not a substitute for the bare Act.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Sub-section (1): who can apply.</strong> A person who has reason to believe that he may be arrested on an accusation of a non-bailable offence can apply to the High Court or the Court of Session. That court may, if it thinks fit, direct that if he is arrested, he shall be released on bail.</li>
<li><strong>Sub-section (2): conditions.</strong> The court may include conditions suited to the facts. The section names three. The person must be available for police questioning when required. He must not threaten or induce anyone who knows the facts. He must not leave India without the court's permission. The court may also add conditions available under Section 480(3).</li>
<li><strong>Sub-section (3): after arrest.</strong> If the police arrest the person on that accusation and he is ready to give bail, he must be released on bail. If a Magistrate decides to issue a warrant, it must be a bailable warrant, in line with the court's direction.</li>
<li><strong>Sub-section (4): the exception.</strong> The section does not apply to an arrest on an accusation of an offence under Section 65 and Section 70(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. These sections deal with rape of a woman under 16 and gang rape of a woman under 18.</li>
</ol>
<p>The old Section 438 had the same four sub-sections. Its fourth sub-section, added in 2018, referred to the IPC sections on rape of girls under 16 and under 12 and on gang rape of such girls.</p>

<h2>Key Supreme Court principles</h2>
<h3>Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab (1980)</h3>
<p>A five-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court decided this case on 9 April 1980. It remains the main authority on anticipatory bail. The Court explained several points:</p>
<ul>
<li>The applicant must have "reason to believe" he may be arrested. A mere vague fear is not enough.</li>
<li>An FIR is not a condition for applying. Anticipatory bail can also be granted after an FIR, as long as the person has not been arrested.</li>
<li>Once a person is arrested for the offence, the remedy is regular bail, not anticipatory bail.</li>
<li>A "blanket order", covering any offence at all, should not normally be passed. The order should name the offence it covers.</li>
<li>The court should decide the application itself. It should not leave the question to the Magistrate.</li>
<li>The normal rule is not to limit the order to a fixed time.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2020)</h3>
<p>This is another five-judge Constitution Bench decision, delivered on 29 January 2020. It answered two questions that had divided different benches.</p>
<ul>
<li>Protection under the section should not always or ordinarily be limited to a fixed period.</li>
<li>The order does not generally end when the accused is summoned or when charges are framed. It can continue until the end of the trial. The court may limit it if the case has special features.</li>
<li>The application should state concrete facts and the reasons for fearing arrest. It may be filed even before an FIR.</li>
<li>Courts should look at the nature and gravity of the offence, the role of the applicant, the chance of influencing the investigation or tampering with evidence, and the chance of fleeing.</li>
<li>The order does not stop the police from investigating. The police can ask the court to cancel the protection if a condition is broken.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Conditions and the court's discretion</h2>
<p>Anticipatory bail is not a right. The court decides after hearing both sides, and the facts of the particular case matter. The Supreme Court has said that extra restrictive conditions should not be imposed in a routine way in every case. They depend on what the investigating agency places before the court.</p>
<p>Common conditions follow Section 482(2): cooperate with the police, do not influence witnesses, and do not leave India without permission. See <a href="/anticipatory-bail-conditions-pune/">anticipatory bail conditions in Pune</a> for more on conditions.</p>

<h2>Anticipatory bail and regular bail</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Timing.</strong> Anticipatory bail is sought before arrest. Regular bail is sought after arrest.</li>
<li><strong>Court.</strong> Anticipatory bail is decided only by the Sessions Court or the High Court. Regular bail can also be decided by a Magistrate in many cases.</li>
<li><strong>Effect.</strong> Anticipatory bail does not stop the arrest. It means release on bail when arrest happens. Regular bail ends custody that has already begun.</li>
<li><strong>Section.</strong> Anticipatory bail is Section 482 BNSS (old 438). Regular bail is mostly Section 480 BNSS (old 437) and Section 483 BNSS (old 439).</li>
</ul>
<p>For the regular route, read <a href="/practice-areas/bail-application/">bail application</a> and <a href="/bail-after-arrest-pune-guide/">bail after arrest in Pune</a>.</p>

<h2>Maharashtra: what we could confirm</h2>
<p>Maharashtra had its own version of Section 438 CrPC, in force since a 1993 state amendment (Maharashtra Act 24 of 1993). It had extra features, such as notice to the Public Prosecutor and the police, and a power to require the applicant to attend the final hearing. The Bombay High Court gave guidelines on interim protection under that version in 2021.</p>
<p>Section 482 BNSS, as published, is a single central provision. We could not confirm from a court ruling whether the old Maharashtra features still apply after 1 July 2024. For a pending or new application, check the current position with an advocate.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>What is Section 438 of CrPC?</h3>
<p>Section 438 CrPC was the section on anticipatory bail. It let a person who feared arrest for a non-bailable offence apply to the High Court or the Court of Session for a direction to be released on bail if arrested.</p>

<h3>Is Section 438 CrPC still in force?</h3>
<p>No. The CrPC was repealed and replaced by the BNSS on 1 July 2024. The same provision is now Section 482 BNSS. Older cases, judgments and FIRs still refer to Section 438.</p>

<h3>What is the anticipatory bail section in the new law?</h3>
<p>It is Section 482 BNSS. Its wording is almost the same as Section 438 CrPC. The cross-references now point to Section 480(3), and the exception in sub-section (4) refers to BNS Sections 65 and 70(2).</p>

<h3>Does Section 438 apply to offences before 1 July 2024?</h3>
<p>Offences committed before that date are still tried under the old laws. Which law governs a fresh bail application in such a case can depend on the facts, so ask an advocate before you file.</p>

<h3>Can I apply under Section 482 BNSS before an FIR is registered?</h3>
<p>The Supreme Court has said an FIR is not a condition. You must still show clear facts that give you real reason to fear arrest. A general worry about being named is not enough.</p>

<h3>Does anticipatory bail have a time limit?</h3>
<p>The Supreme Court has said it should not ordinarily be limited to a fixed period. It can continue until the end of the trial. The court may limit it if special features of the case require this.</p>

<h3>What is the difference between Section 438 and Sections 437 and 439 CrPC?</h3>
<p>Section 438 gave bail before arrest. Sections 437 and 439 deal with bail after arrest. Under the BNSS, these are Sections 482, 480 and 483.</p>

<p><strong>In short:</strong> Anticipatory bail (also called pre-arrest bail, or "agrim jamanat" in Hindi and Marathi) is a court order that you get before arrest. It says that if the police arrest you in a named case, they must release you on bail. In Pune you can apply to the Pune District and Sessions Court or to the Bombay High Court in Mumbai, under Section 482 BNSS (old Section 438 CrPC).</p>

<h2>Anticipatory bail meaning in plain words</h2>
<p>Normally you ask for bail after arrest. Anticipatory bail works the other way round. You ask the court in advance, because you have reason to believe you may be arrested for a non-bailable offence.</p>
<p>If the court grants it, the arrest does not lead to jail. The police can still arrest you, but you must be released on bail on the terms the court has set.</p>
<p>It does not cancel the FIR and it does not stop the investigation. You remain an accused until the case ends or is quashed. For a bailable offence, bail is a right at the police station, so this order is usually not needed.</p>

<h2>Section 482 BNSS (old Section 438 CrPC)</h2>
<p>The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure on 1 July 2024. Anticipatory bail is now in Section 482 BNSS. The old number was Section 438 CrPC, which is why many people still search for "anticipatory bail CrPC" or "sec 438 of CrPC".</p>
<p>The core wording is the same. A person who has reason to believe he may be arrested on an accusation of a non-bailable offence can apply to the High Court or the Court of Session. Offences from before 1 July 2024 are still dealt with under the old laws, so an older FIR may still quote IPC sections. See our <a href="/crpc-to-bnss-ipc-to-bns-section-table/">CrPC to BNSS section table</a> for old and new numbers.</p>
<p>For step-by-step practical detail, read <a href="/how-to-get-anticipatory-bail-pune/">how to get anticipatory bail in Pune</a>.</p>

<h2>Who can apply, and when</h2>
<p>The Supreme Court has said that the fear of arrest must rest on real facts, not on a vague worry. An FIR is not required. You can apply before an FIR if the facts clearly show why you expect to be arrested. You can also apply after an FIR, as long as you have not been arrested.</p>
<p>Anticipatory bail is usually considered in situations like these:</p>
<ul>
<li>An FIR has been registered and you are named as an accused.</li>
<li>You have received a police notice under <a href="/practice-areas/police-notice-section-35-bnss/">Section 35(3) BNSS</a> (old Section 41A CrPC) and fear arrest after questioning.</li>
<li>A complaint is likely against you, for example in a matrimonial dispute (see <a href="/practice-areas/498a-domestic-violence-defence/">498A defence</a>), a property dispute or a money dispute.</li>
<li>The police have visited your home or workplace, or have called your family, in connection with a case.</li>
</ul>
<p>Once you are arrested, anticipatory bail is no longer available for that offence. You then need regular bail. See <a href="/practice-areas/bail-application/">bail application</a> and <a href="/bail-after-arrest-pune-guide/">bail after arrest in Pune</a>.</p>
<p>Not sure whether an FIR exists? Read <a href="/how-to-check-fir-registered-against-you-maharashtra/">how to check if an FIR is registered against you</a>.</p>

</ul>

<h2>Anticipatory bail procedure: the stages</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Study of the case.</strong> The FIR, notice or complaint is read. The offences are checked to see whether they are non-bailable and which court should hear the matter.</li>
<li><strong>Application.</strong> A written application is filed in the chosen court. It contains the facts of the matter, the reason you fear arrest, and your own version. Supporting papers are attached. We do not offer a fill-in format, because each application must fit its own facts.</li>
<li><strong>Interim protection.</strong> The court may issue notice to the Public Prosecutor and, if it thinks fit, give short-term protection from arrest until the final hearing. This is called interim anticipatory bail. It is not automatic.</li>
<li><strong>Hearing.</strong> The Public Prosecutor, usually with inputs from the investigating officer, opposes or responds. The court hears you and decides.</li>
<li><strong>Order.</strong> The court grants the application with conditions, or refuses it. If granted, the order applies if you are arrested. You may be asked to furnish a bond, with or without sureties.</li>
</ol>
<p>Section 482 sets no fixed time limit for the court to decide. How long it takes depends on the court, the offence and the listing of the matter.</p>

<h2>What an application contains</h2>
<p>The Supreme Court has said that an anticipatory bail application should state the essential facts of the offence, why the applicant reasonably fears arrest, and the applicant's version of events. A general plea such as "I may be falsely implicated sometime" is not enough.</p>
<p>In general, a good application covers these points:</p>
<ul>
<li>The FIR or complaint, or the facts that make arrest likely.</li>
<li>The sections alleged and why they are non-bailable.</li>
<li>Your role as alleged, and your answer to it.</li>
<li>Whether you are cooperating with the police, including any notice received.</li>
<li>Your address, work and family ties, which bear on the risk of absconding.</li>
<li>Whether you have any earlier case, and whether any earlier bail application was made.</li>
<li>The conditions you are ready to accept.</li>
</ul>
<p>People often search for an "anticipatory bail format", "draft" or "application format". Copying a format without these facts weakens the application. Always have a lawyer check the draft against the FIR.</p>

<h2>What the court considers</h2>
<p>Anticipatory bail is a matter of the court's discretion. In <em>Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab</em> (1980), a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court said that courts weigh several things. These include the nature and seriousness of the accusation, the context of the events, the chance that the applicant will not be available for trial, and the risk that witnesses will be tampered with. The Court also said that if the accusation seems aimed at humiliating the applicant through arrest, bail would generally be granted.</p>
<p>In <em>Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi)</em> (2020), another Constitution Bench said courts should look at the nature and gravity of the offence, the role of the applicant, the chance of influencing the investigation or tampering with evidence, and the chance of fleeing justice.</p>

<h2>Typical conditions</h2>
<p>Section 482(2) BNSS lets the court attach conditions suited to the case. The section lists these:</p>
<ul>
<li>Make yourself available for questioning by the police when required.</li>
<li>Do not threaten, induce or make promises to anyone who knows the facts, so as to stop them telling the court or the police.</li>
<li>Do not leave India without the court's permission.</li>
<li>Other conditions that a court can impose on bail under Section 480(3) BNSS. These include attending court as per the bond, not committing a similar offence, and not tampering with evidence.</li>
</ul>
<p>A court may add other conditions in a particular case. Read the order carefully and follow every term. For more detail, see <a href="/anticipatory-bail-conditions-pune/">anticipatory bail conditions in Pune</a>.</p>

<h2>Validity: how long does anticipatory bail last?</h2>
<p>In <em>Sushila Aggarwal</em>, the Supreme Court held that protection should not ordinarily be limited to a fixed period. It does not generally end when the chargesheet is filed, when you are summoned, or when charges are framed. It can continue until the end of the trial.</p>
<p>The court can still limit the duration if the case has special features. The order also stays tied to the offence named in it. It does not cover a new offence. The police can ask the court to cancel the protection if you break a condition, for example by not cooperating or by threatening witnesses. Read <a href="/chargesheet-filed-what-next/">chargesheet filed: what next</a> for the stage after investigation.</p>

<h2>If anticipatory bail is refused</h2>
<p>A refusal is not the end of the road. You can apply to the Bombay High Court in Mumbai, which also has power under Section 482. The High Court looks at the Sessions Court's reasons and the facts again. Read <a href="/bail-rejected-sessions-court-pune-what-next/">bail rejected by the Sessions Court: what next</a>.</p>
<p>If you are arrested in the meantime, regular bail is the remedy. Do not ignore a court date or a police notice while you wait.</p>

<h2>Mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Waiting until the police are at the door. Plan the application early.</li>
<li>Skipping a notice under Section 35(3) BNSS. The notice must be followed. See <a href="/practice-areas/police-notice-section-35-bnss/">police notice under Section 35 BNSS</a>.</li>
<li>Using a downloaded format that does not match your FIR.</li>
<li>Leaving out facts, such as an earlier case, that the other side will raise.</li>
<li>Breaking a condition after bail is granted. This can lead to cancellation.</li>
<li>Contacting the complainant or witnesses to "settle" matters while the case is pending.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Agrim jamanat kya hota hai?</h3>
<p>Agrim jamanat is the Hindi and Marathi term for anticipatory bail. It is a court order given before arrest. If the police arrest you in the case named in the order, they must release you on bail on the court's terms.</p>

<h3>What is the difference between anticipatory bail and regular bail?</h3>
<p>Anticipatory bail is sought before arrest, from the Sessions Court or the High Court. Regular bail is sought after arrest, and a Magistrate's court can also grant it. See <a href="/practice-areas/bail-application/">bail application</a> for regular bail.</p>

<h3>Can I apply for anticipatory bail before an FIR is registered?</h3>
<p>Yes, in some cases. The Supreme Court has said an FIR is not a condition. But you must show clear facts that give you reason to fear arrest. A vague worry will not do.</p>

<h3>What is interim anticipatory bail?</h3>
<p>It is short-term protection from arrest that a court may give while the main application is pending. The court usually issues notice to the Public Prosecutor and fixes a date for the final hearing. It is not granted automatically.</p>

<h3>Is there an anticipatory bail format or draft I can download?</h3>
<p>Formats are easy to find, but an application works only if it states your facts and the reason for your fear of arrest. A copied draft often misses these points. A lawyer should prepare it from your FIR or notice.</p>

<h3>How long is anticipatory bail valid?</h3>
<p>The Supreme Court has said the protection need not have a fixed end date. It can continue after the chargesheet and until the end of the trial. The court can limit it in special cases, and the order can be cancelled if conditions are broken.</p>

<h3>Is pre-arrest bail the same as anticipatory bail?</h3>
<p>Yes. "Pre-arrest bail" and "anticipatory bail" mean the same thing. The law now calls it a direction under Section 482 BNSS (old Section 438 CrPC).</p>

<h3>Where do I apply in Pune, and what if the Sessions Court refuses?</h3>
<p>You can apply to the Pune District and Sessions Court or to the Bombay High Court in Mumbai. If the Sessions Court refuses, you can go to the High Court. There is no Pune bench of the Bombay High Court.</p>
